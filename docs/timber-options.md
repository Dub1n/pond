# timber options – design C

> Readily sourceable framing and decking wood choices mapped to design-C.md (C24, UC4, 47×150 joists/beams; 28×145 decking; 400 mm c/c).

---

## Overview & Assumptions

- Location context: UK/EU merchants; EN 338 strength classes (C24) and EN 335 Use Classes (UC4 for framing per design-C).
- Members: joists/beams/blocking 47×150; decking target 28×145; joist spacing 400 mm c/c; beams on pads/DPC; pond-edge moisture risk high.
- Goal: balance structural performance, availability in required sections, durability near water, and price; preserve the low-profile, flush-hanger details.

---

## Framing (joists, beams, blocking)

Primary recommendation (aligns with design-C “UC4 throughout”):

- C24 European Redwood (Scots Pine), UC4 incised
  - Why: Consistently graded to C24; excellent preservative uptake to UC4 (incised), giving reliable durability near the pond.
  - Availability: 47×150 regularised widely stocked; typical lengths 3.0, 3.6, 4.8, 6.0 m. Easy to splice over supports where needed.
  - Cost: Low–medium (usually the best-value path to C24 + UC4).
  - Notes: Specify “C24 47×150 UC4 incised (brown/green)”. Many yards default to UC3 unless UC4 is requested—call it out.

Acceptable alternatives (order of preference), with notes:

- C24 Douglas Fir (European), UC4 where certified
  - Strong and stiff; straight. Treatment to UC4 is hit-or-miss—confirm certificate. Availability in 47×150 is good; UC4 stock may be special order.
  - Cost: Medium–high.

- Structural European Larch, UC3b/limited UC4
  - Tough and naturally more durable than spruce; treatability to UC4 is inconsistent. Often rougher and more movement; visually fine for hidden framing.
  - Cost: Medium–high. Prefer pine UC4 at the inner beam if larch cannot be UC4 certified.

- C24 Spruce/Whitewood, UC3b (only if you must deviate from UC4)
  - Very common and cheap; poor treatability to UC4. Use only if fully isolated (DPC over pads, good airflow, no splashback) and you accept relaxing the design’s UC4 requirement. Not advised at the pond edge.
  - Cost: Low.

Engineered options (not required for spans/supports here):

- Exterior-rated LVL/glulam
  - Delivers straightness and capacity but adds cost and treatment/adhesive considerations around UC4 exposure. Overkill for this deck.

Framing bottom line:

- Use “C24 47×150 UC4 incised pine” for all joists, both beams, and blocking per design-C. Keep tops perfectly flush for saddle/face hangers.
- Where length is short, splice directly over supports; add solid blocking tight either side; maintain hanger seat alignment.
- Inner beam sits closest to water: prioritise UC4 incised pine there even if other members vary.

## Decking (wood options, 28×145 target)

Value choice (fully compatible with 400 mm c/c):

- Treated Softwood (European Redwood Pine), UC3b
  - Look: Classic softwood deck; can be left to weather or stained; will silver/green depending on finish.
  - Board sizes: 28×145 commonly stocked; matches current joist spacing.
  - Durability: Above-ground UC3b is suitable with your 1–2% fall and ventilation. Reseal cut ends.
  - Cost: Low (cheapest route).

Mid-premium natural option:

- Siberian Larch (naturally durable softwood)
  - Look: Warm gold to silver-grey; fine grain; contemporary aesthetic.
  - Board sizes: 27–28×145 is common; works at 400 mm c/c.
  - Durability: Natural Durability Class ≈3 above ground; no chemical treatment needed; good near water with drainage.
  - Cost: Medium–high (above treated pine, below modified wood/hardwoods).

Premium stability-first options (modified softwoods):

- Accoya or Kebony
  - Look: Clean, uniform; weathers evenly to silver if uncoated; takes finishes exceptionally well.
  - Board sizes: Often ~25×150 (varies); many systems span ~400 mm—confirm supplier span tables.
  - Durability: Class 1; very stable near water. Seal cuts with the system’s end-grain product.
  - Cost: High.

High-end hardwood options:

- Ipe, Cumaru, Garapa (select by color/tone)
  - Look: Dense, luxurious; rich browns/golds, weather to silver. Pre-drilling required; A4 stainless screws.
  - Board sizes: Often 19–21×140/145; many species can span 400 mm thanks to density—check supplier data.
  - Durability: Class 1–2; excellent longevity near water. Confirm certified sourcing.
  - Cost: High–very high.

Selective lightweight option (only if thickness increased or spacing reduced):

- Western Red Cedar
  - Look: Beautiful grain and color; soft underfoot; silvers rapidly.
  - Board sizes: 19–21×140 typical; at that thickness, prefer 300 mm c/c or choose thicker boards for 400 mm c/c.
  - Durability: Class 2 above ground; softer surface dents more easily.
  - Cost: Medium–high.

## Joist Spacing Compatibility (rules of thumb)

- 28×145 treated softwood: 400 mm c/c OK (as designed).
- 27–28×145 Siberian Larch: 400 mm c/c typically OK.
- ~25×150 modified wood (Accoya/Kebony): 400 mm c/c usually OK; confirm manufacturer tables.
- 19–21 mm hardwood/cedar: verify span tables; hardwoods often OK at 400 mm; cedar often needs 300 mm or thicker boards.

---

## Hardware, Treatment & Durability Notes

- Fasteners/hangers: Stainless A2 minimum; A4 preferred by the pond and for hardwoods/modified woods. Follow hanger/strap nail schedules.
- Cut ends: Reseal all cuts on treated softwood (and use system-specific end-grain sealers for Accoya/Kebony).
- Isolation: DPC or EPDM between timber and concrete pads; isolate metal edges from liner with EPDM/underlay.
- Drainage: Maintain 1–2% fall away from pond; 5–6 mm board gaps; underside drip kerf ~10–15 mm in from outer edge.
- Inner beam: Highest moisture risk—use UC4 incised pine and keep all fixings above waterline.

## Purchasing Checklist (ready to order)

- Framing stock: “C24, 47×150 regularised, UC4 incised pine (European Redwood), brown/green; lengths 3.6–4.8 m; same section for solid blocking.”
- Hangers/straps: Top-flange saddle hangers (inner, rebated); face-mount joist hangers (outer); LSTA-type top straps for uplift; galvanised flat strap for X-bracing (30–40 mm × 1–1.5 mm). Stainless/class 4 fasteners per manufacturer.
- Pads/DPC: Concrete pads 300×300×100; DPC/EPDM strips at beam interfaces; breathable weed membrane under deck.
- Decking (pick one):
  - Value: “28×145 treated softwood decking (UC3b), smooth; stainless screws; reseal cuts.”
  - Mid: “27–28×145 Siberian Larch decking, smooth; stainless screws.”
  - Premium: “~25×150 Accoya or Kebony decking; approved screws; end-grain sealer.”
  - High-end: “21×145 Ipe/Cumaru/Garapa; A4 stainless; pre-drill.”

## Bottom Line

- Frame it all in UC4 incised pine (C24 47×150) to match design-C’s durability brief, with special attention to the inner beam near the pond.
- Choose decking to taste and budget: treated softwood for value; Siberian larch for natural silvering; Accoya/Kebony for stability and low movement; tropical hardwoods for maximum durability and premium aesthetics.
