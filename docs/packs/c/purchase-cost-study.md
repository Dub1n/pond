## What the recent decisions establish

The recent chats and commits show a consistent method:

- `9c4dd1a` replaced the fitted suspension with 36 direct cord items and
  replaced solid stainless seat sleeves with user-bored acetal.
- The compact corner was shaped around an economical stock width. The later
  upper corner/basket integration removed four separate R5.1 cradles.
- `0ad0993` established the continuous corner-seat plate; the subsequent
  `t=30` work and `b85fd49` selected the thick Clam/lower-web profiles and
  nested all 35 parts into the common-sheet purchase.
- `dc628c4` separated the E23 comparison properties from actual sheet evidence.
  A product described as flooring is not automatically the structural sheet
  used in the calculation.

The useful lesson is to price an entire workable assembly, including order
rounding. Inherited dimensions, hardware grades and adjustment fittings are
open to change. Retain a feature when its duty or the cost comparison supports
it, rather than treating the historical specification as a constraint.

## What changes, and why it is adequate to prototype

### Ordinary freshwater stainless, not a marine purchasing specification

A2-70 bolts and A2 washers/nuts are the first purchase candidate. Cold
freshwater is a materially different duty from deliberately saline water;
[BSSA's water-service guidance](https://bssa.org.uk/bssa_articles/category-water-water-treatment/)
supports considering 304 as well as 316. This does not mean all freshwater
chemistries are identical or that crevice/deposit corrosion is impossible.
Inspect the submerged threads and spacers after wet testing and periodically
in service. Do not substitute zinc-plated steel, brass or unidentified plated
job-lot hardware on the strength of its shiny appearance.

The reason to spend money here is to obtain known material, fit and smooth
bearing surfaces, **not** a marine logo. Identifiable unused A2 job lots are
valid substitutes if they meet the same count/fit requirements and beat the
whole order price. Old rope, damaged threads and unidentified coatings are
less useful bargains. The selected 68 M8 bolts and nuts together cost only
GBP 23.69; wholesale joint redesign has little remaining cash to recover.

Nylocs replace expensive all-metal locknuts. Low tightening load, witness
marks, wet-dwell inspection and subsequent checks remain required. Do not
assume a repeatedly removed nylon insert retains its original locking action.

### GRP–GRP connections: stock bolts and simple spacers

Keep the 24 corner bolts, 36 seat bolts and their existing axes. At the
9.5 mm sheet basis, a corner structural stack is 9.5 + 38 + 9.5 = 57 mm;
the nominal 39 mm seat gap gives a 58 mm seat stack. Two 1.5 mm washers make
the nut-bearing distances 60 and 61 mm. At 10 mm plate these become 61 and
62 mm. The M8 x 80 DIN 931 listing has 22 mm of thread, leaving 58 mm
nominal plain shank, so it can tighten these stacks. In contrast, M8 x 90
with the same threaded length leaves 68 mm plain shank and cannot tighten
them without added packing. Buying longer was not automatically safer.

The new detail deliberately permits the thread transition in the lower GRP
plate, rather than demanding a custom grip length. Screening the full existing
0.763 kN corner-bolt force against a conservative 6.5 mm thread-root diameter
and 9.5 mm plate gives 12.4 MPa nominal bearing. This is below the E23 dry
70 MPa comparator in [materials.md](materials.md),
but that comparison does not certify the purchased laminate's wet bearing
strength. Coupon-test slip, hole damage and stiffness with the actual bolt;
no friction resistance from an assumed tightening torque is credited.

The 12 x 1 mm steel tube is a **crush spacer**, not a close-fit shear bushing.
Its 10 mm bore is therefore acceptable to investigate around an M8 bolt;
the GRP bolt holes stay 8.2–8.5 mm. Its annular compression area is about
34.6 mm2. Cut 24 nominal 28 mm lengths to the measured internal rail cavity,
square and deburr them, and establish gentle assembly torque by coupon.
Do not infer an acceptable preload from the 0.763 kN in-plane bolt force.
Do not enlarge the rail holes to 12 mm to push spacers through: insert them
from the open mitred ends.

Make 36 seat spacers from the 16 mm acetal rod: 39 mm nominal length,
8.2–8.5 mm bore, as already developed. The sleeve/rail relationship and
four-bolt capture remain unchanged. Unfilled acetal is a plausible candidate
for its low water uptake and rigidity; [Ensinger's POM-C material information](https://www.ensingerplastics.com/en-us/shapes/acetal-tecaform-ah-natural)
is comparative evidence, not certification of Direct Plastics' particular
batch. Confirm unfilled acetal grade on supply.

### Cord–GRP connections: eight separate acetal-covered posts

Use eight separate M8 x 50 bolts through Arrangement E's C2 ear axes, not
eight of the 24 corner bolts. Over each bolt put a smooth 16 mm OD acetal
post, initially **20 mm high**, retained by a 30 mm OD roof washer under the
bolt head; put a 25 mm washer and nyloc below the plate. Bore the post
8.2–8.5 mm and round/polish the rope-bearing edges. The rope wraps the acetal
through its replaceable chafe sleeve, never the thread. The acetal bears
directly on the GRP; this post clamp does not preload a rail/corner joint.

At 10 mm plate, the length needed is 1.5 roof + 20 post + 10 plate +
1.5 underside washer + 8 nut + 2.5 two-thread allowance = **43.5 mm**.
M8 x 50 allows fitting tolerance and trimming. Its 28 mm nominal plain
shank ends inside the plate, before the nut-bearing face at 33 mm, so it
can clamp. M8 x 40 was explored but would force a roughly 16 mm-high post
and leave almost no length tolerance merely to save 24 pence on eight bolts.

The 30 mm roof projects 7 mm radially beyond the 16 mm post; the previous
25 mm roof gives only 4.5 mm, less than the new bare 5 mm cord diameter.
Check the actual sleeved turn, knot, basket and removable-deck template;
post height remains adjustable within the purchased rod and bolt stock.
The bend diameter is modest (16/5 = 3.2), so chafe and knot seating require
wet proof. A conservative 0.30 kN acting 20 mm above the plate gives a
6 N m bolt-root moment; local bolt bending, plate bearing, washer prying and
acetal creep must be checked in the assembled test, not dismissed because
the bolt's axial rating is large.

### Cord: relax strength purchasing, still measure stretch

The 5 mm reel has a supplier-published **approximate** breaking load of
310 kg, about 3.04 kN. This replaces the old 6 mm / 6 kN buying specification.
It is not a certified working-load limit. Against a 0.30 kN complete-item
proof, even allowing a provisional 50% strength loss at a knot gives a
useful screening margin; the precise knot loss is not known for this cord.
The supplier publishes no stiffness that qualifies the completed loops.

The existing model's `EA_LOOP = 6000 N` is **effective axial rigidity**, not
the rope's breaking load. Preserve that distinction. After bedding, measure
complete-item extension over the working load band, approximately 10–120 N,
and calculate `EA_eff = L * delta(T) / delta(extension)`. To retain the
existing stiffness input it must reach 6,000 N per complete equivalent loop
(the model divides this between two C2 legs). Otherwise rerun using the
measured value and check rail movement against the actual clearance/level
requirements; do not reject a cord merely because it differs from the old
assumption. A 300 N proof alone does not establish stiffness.

The extra GBP 11.04 for 5 mm rather than the listed 4 mm buys roughly twice
the supplier-published breaking load and a larger wear/contact section while
still fitting the target. It does not buy a demonstrated stiffness result.
If 4 mm passes the same measured functional checks, there is no reason in
this study to insist on 5 mm.

The historical ring screen gives 94.5 N maximum in the listed normal cases
and about 117.2 N with one complete item lost. Its 5.02 mm displacement is
not a result for this product or final Arrangement E geometry. Simply
scaling area from 6 to 5 mm would make it about 7.22 mm, but this is only
a sensitivity illustration, not a prediction of the chosen braid's behaviour.
Use measured current tangents and settled cord behaviour before release.

The separate 3 mm PET reel has no published breaking load. It is selected
only for locating collars and crossing separators/tags, not for suspending
the rail. Seat-stop lashings are superseded by the existing four-bolt clamp,
with optional pad or adhesive assistance. Do not cut seat-lashing slots or
grooves. Follow the sample fitting and 0.10 kN dry/wet slide tests in
[install-walkthrough.md](install-walkthrough.md); the mechanical capture stays
in place with every interface option.

### Basket retention and finishing

Keep all 52 button screws and 40 mm internal washers. These are ordinary
A2 rather than A4; the broad mesh contact is useful even when bolt strength
is not governing. Keep one underside Form G washer and nyloc per screw.
Do not replace four distributed base-rib contacts with a narrow tie around
one mesh strand merely to save pennies. The bought internal washers cost
GBP 10.64 for the entire system; see the offcut alternative below.

No separate tags or caps are bought. Mark component/cord IDs on existing
parts or small sealed plate offcuts and tie with the locating cord. Trial
assemble, mark the required bolt length, then remove, cut and round the
ends outside the pond. Preserve full nut/nylon engagement and the required
thread emergence, but remove excess tails. Face nut ends into sheltered
locations and test with the actual bag and a snag cloth. If a necessary
thread end remains accessible and snags, a guard/cap is required; the
GBP 498.92 figure then needs updating. Caps are not a structural necessity,
but a smooth finished assembly is.

The EL2 kit is a cut-edge sealer only, conditional on compatibility with the
supplied GRP resin. Its [manufacturer TDS](https://media.easycomposites.co.uk/datasheets/EC-TDS-EL2-Epoxy-Laminating-Resin.pdf)
distinguishes handling cure from full mechanical cure (14 days at 25 C).
Fully cure and rinse before immersion; a shorter demould time is not pond
readiness. The product is not presented here as aquarium-certified. Obtain
the GRP/resin supplier's suitability guidance rather than claiming every
fully cured adhesive is automatically suitable for fish. Mixing containers,
applicators and PPE are assumed existing workshop consumables like the
already owner-provided abrasives/tools; if absent, add their purchase cost.

## Assembly acceptance shared by the cheaper options

Use the existing measurable functional gates:

- Support knots, wraps and C2 assemblies: 0.30 kN complete-item proof over dry
  ground, then wet dwell and retest, with recorded tail and location movement.
- Rope: strength alone is insufficient. Measure settled stretch over the
  working load range and use it in the ring model; include both legs of a
  closed loop and remove both C2 legs together in a sling-loss case.
- Corner: at least 20 kN m/rad after bedding, including the 60 N m plan and
  30 N m roll actions and the revised C2/basket load paths.
- Seat stops: 0.10 kN along the rail in each direction, five cycles and
  ten-minute holds, repeated after seven days wet; at most 1 mm permanent
  migration and no damage. Replacing a buckle does not relax this test.
- Basket attachments: preserve four distributed sound-rib bearing locations,
  check one-corner uplift and removal, and avoid a narrow tie cutting into a
  single mesh strand.
- Finish: no exposed glass fibres, sharp metal or accessible projecting
  threads; all knots and wear surfaces remain inspectable and replaceable.

These are the existing prototype duties, not a new requirement to buy
certified marine fittings. The full ring/deck checks remain separate from
local connection proof.
