## 1. Independent pre-read view

The system must support 13 independently removable baskets at several crown depths, without touching the liner, and carry their full soaked weight with the pond drained. Crowns need open sky beyond the deck overhang, while baskets and structure remain submerged and visually recessive. It must resist eccentric loading, twisting, plant leverage, ordinary movement and handling uplift while preserving the central pond.

Reasonable concepts were:

- Modular twin-rail side carriers with removable, height-adjustable cradles.
- Individual cantilevered or suspended cradles from joist ends.
- A modular suspended perimeter frame.
- Tensioned cable or rope carriers, considered less attractive because of sag, chafe and tuning.

My provisional preference was modular twin-rail side carriers. They distribute loads, restrain basket rotation naturally, allow standardized height modules and permit inward sliding and independent recovery without occupying the centre.

The controlling unknowns were the surveyed geometry, real basket bases and reinforced zones, completed soaked masses, planting-pot/crown geometry, rail bending and torsion, hanger reactions, failure of one support, creep and chafe, and a full-scale removal test beneath a mock deck edge.

## 2. What `rail.md` proposes

R3 is a 2,100 × 2,100 mm square ring of 38 mm structural GRP box section. Its centreline is 450 mm from each pond wall—nominally 100 mm beyond the deck edge—and it hangs from 32 adjustable polyester-cord supports, one at every straight joist.

Each basket bears on two short 25 mm GRP crossbars laid across the single ring spine. Large baskets sit directly on them; all small baskets receive nominal 38–40 mm risers. Four cord ties retain each basket. The bearing plane is about 200 mm below minimum water and the main rail about 225 mm below it. Mitred corners use bolted top-and-bottom GRP L-plates.

The document treats this as a survey and prototype candidate, not an issued fabrication design.

## 3. Verdict

**Prototype but do not yet accept.**

R3 is credible enough to prototype and contains several good decisions, but it presently fails one explicit planting-depth requirement, does not demonstrate whole-system torsional and lateral behaviour, and leaves important maintenance and flooding details unresolved.

## 4. Comparison with my independent preference

Reading the proposal improved my view of the single-spine option. Local transverse seats are more material-efficient than two complete perimeter rails, and adapting them to measured tapered basket bases is sensible. The rigid ring also gives some global lateral restraint that four unconnected side rails would need to reproduce deliberately.

It has not displaced my preference for twin-rail side modules. The single spine relies on basket balance, cord bindings and the ring corners for rotational stability; the proposal then introduces 32 main supports and roughly 142 additional bindings, ties and collars. That is cheaper in hardware than many stainless hangers, but not obviously simpler to assemble, inspect or renew.

The right decision is now empirical: prototype the single-spine arrangement with an optional second stabilizing rail and compare them under identical asymmetric and support-loss tests.

## 5. Strongest aspects

- **Plan geometry is fundamentally sound.** At the nominal datum, a 230 mm basket overlaps the deck by about 15 mm and a 270–280 mm basket by 35–40 mm. The stated 215 mm and 235–240 mm open portions are correct.
- A centred 150 mm planting pot begins about 25 mm beyond the nominal deck edge, so it has open sky in the strict geometric sense.
- The proposal correctly designs for full drained weight, not favourable submerged weight.
- Using every joist gives low rail bending demand and useful redundancy.
- Direct GRP bearing carries gravity load; the ties are not incorrectly credited as primary suspension.
- Actual basket bases, masses, water levels and joist geometry are explicitly identified as hold points.
- The cut-edge, fastener and sealant treatment is thoughtful. Strongwell likewise advises sealing exposed reinforcement and warns about local bolt stresses, washers and tapped FRP threads. [Strongwell fabrication manual](https://www.strongwell.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Strongwell-Fabrication-and-Repair-Manual.pdf)
- The document correctly gives Gold Label no structural credit. Current instructions describe it as a sealant that needs a substantial bead, not a compressed glue line. [Gold Label instructions](https://www.huttonaquaticproducts.co.uk/faqs/)
- The bare ring fits through the finished opening, and fitting seats after lowering is a workable fallback.

## 6. Problems and uncertainties

### Blocking

1. **The stated vertical arrangement does not serve every plant.**

   - Large-basket rims are approximately 10 mm below minimum water. Placing the *Butomus* crown “at or just below” that rim gives roughly 10–perhaps 30 mm water cover, not the required 100–150 mm. The outer basket is deep enough to accommodate a lower internal pot, but that pot geometry has not been defined.
   - Small-basket rims are approximately 10–12 mm below water. Recessing crowfoot only 10–25 mm below the rim gives approximately 20–37 mm water above its rooting point, not 200–350 mm.
   - With its 40 mm risers, the small basket base is only about 160 mm below water. The crowfoot cannot reach a 200 mm minimum rooting depth within that arrangement. It needs a dedicated downstand seat or hanging cradle.

2. **Whole-system stability remains unproved.** The arithmetic checks vertical beam bending only. It does not quantify:

   - twisting of a single spine under eccentric contents;
   - the approximately 5 N·m torque created by moving a 100 N basket 50 mm inward;
   - ring racking and corner-plate response;
   - lateral reactions from inclined cords;
   - plant leverage;
   - behaviour after loss of an end or corner support.

   The corner cord has a plan offset of about 203 mm from its joist attachment. Its horizontal-to-vertical reaction ratio is therefore approximately `203 / actual vertical drop`, which cannot be dismissed until the drop is surveyed.

3. **Later replacement of the main supports is not resolved.** Cord wrapped around a joist before decking may become inaccessible afterward. Every permanent support needs either a removable deck-access provision or an A4 through-fastened termination designed for replacement from an accessible side.

4. **The mitred ring lacks an explicit controlled flood-and-drain detail.** The main ring has no open ends once its four corners are assembled. Leakage through imperfect mitres is not a reliable vent. Each side needs deliberate, resin-sealed upper vent and lower drain openings.

5. **Survey, real basket geometry, soaked masses and the deck reaction check remain outstanding.** The document acknowledges these correctly, but they necessarily block fabrication acceptance.

### Important but non-blocking

- The nominal 25 mm clearance between the planting pot and deck edge is modest. Fascia, board overhang, survey error, crown eccentricity or an angled deck edge could consume it.
- Removal travel is marginal at the larger baskets. Their calculated overlap is 35–40 mm, so the stated 20–50 mm movement only works near the top of that range and with additional handling clearance.
- Four lower mesh ties per basket are inspection- and servicing-heavy. Every removal entails cutting and later retying submerged lower points. Their local capacity also depends on unmeasured plastic mesh reinforcement.
- Crossbar bindings and support-wrap collars rely on cord friction. Positive rounded stops should be standard rather than added only after movement is observed.
- The system contains 26 crossbar bindings, 52 basket ties and 64 support collars, in addition to the 32 principal supports. That weakens the claim that it is the simplest maintainable option.
- Direct cord wraps can retain moisture against timber and concentrate bearing near joist ends. Chafe pads may worsen moisture retention; the joist detail needs a timber-durability assessment.
- Rotating the two pultruded corner plates through 90° is sensible, but it does not establish joint capacity. Each direction still depends partly on weak-axis plate properties, bolt bearing and the cut L-shape.
- A basket-position drawing is absent, so opposing-basket clearance, corner crowding, watercress circulation and the intended planting composition cannot yet be checked.
- The current product evidence supports GRP as a material class for wet aquaculture structures, but not necessarily the selected Brundle profile’s resin system or fish-facing suitability. Fiberline describes direct tank use but also says selection remains exposure- and project-dependent. [Fiberline aquaculture guidance](https://fiberline.com/solutions/fish-farming)
- The main ring may be removable after decking only after all baskets and crossbars are removed. Reinstallation of inaccessible upper cord loops would remain problematic.

### Minor improvement

- Define the permitted knot or splice, tail length, inspection criterion and replacement interval.
- Define what the 2 mm movement acceptance limit includes and where it is measured.
- Replace hollow risers where practical; the proposed 20 risers create another 40 cut tube ends requiring drainage and fish-safe protection.
- State explicitly whether 38 × 38 × 3.2 mm or 5 mm is the issued section. Both pass the short-span elastic screen, but their corner and handling behaviour differ.
- Provide a cut-and-kerf schedule before claiming that one 6 m crossbar length will suffice.

## 8. Recommended design

I would next build a **single 2,100 mm full-side comparative prototype**, not the complete ring.

It should include:

- The proposed 38 mm primary spine at the actual cord angles and maximum 420 mm support spacing.
- Four representative loaded stations: shallow B23, shallow B28, *Butomus* B28 and deep crowfoot B23.
- A detachable parallel 25 mm GRP stabilizing rail, positioned beneath the measured basket bases. Test first as the R3 single spine, then as a two-rail ladder under identical loads.
- Positive rounded crossbar locators from the outset.
- A removable basket cradle or two accessible retention straps that do not depend on repeatedly cutting four submerged lower-mesh ties.
- A standardized adjustable downstand cradle for crowfoot, set from measured internal pot geometry to place its rooting point near the middle of the 200–350 mm range.
- A defined internal pot seat for *Butomus* giving 100–150 mm water above its crown.
- Deliberate vent and drain holes in every hollow member; preferably use solid or inherently open sections for the numerous short risers.
- The actual future-accessible joist termination and a representative finished deck-edge/fascia mock-up.

If the single spine remains level and stable during asymmetric loading, a 50 mm inward basket shift, handling uplift and removal of the worst support, retain R3 because it uses less GRP and is less visible. If it rolls, yaws, loosens bindings or depends materially on the completed ring, use four independent twin-rail side modules. Those would cost roughly two additional 6 m main-profile lengths and show one extra submerged line, but would provide direct torsional stability, simpler positive seats, independent side removal and less reliance on cord bindings.

## 9. Immediate next actions

1. Survey the deck edge, fascia, liner, joist tips, water levels and available future access.
2. Buy and measure one basket of each size plus the intended internal pots; record bases, bands, tie zones and crown-to-bearing dimensions.
3. Build, soak and weigh representative completed baskets both underwater and fully drained.
4. Produce one explicit elevation schedule for all 13 plants, including the crowfoot downstand and *Butomus* internal pot.
5. Obtain selected-product GRP resin, longitudinal/transverse properties, immersion approval and connection guidance.
6. Build the comparative side prototype and test full drained load, eccentric loading, twist, uplift, one removed support, creep, chafe, flooding and drainage.
7. Perform the basket-slide-and-lift operation beneath a full-size finished deck-edge mock-up.
8. Choose single- or twin-rail construction from those results, then complete the joist, connection, uplift and foundation checks before issuing any batch cut list.
