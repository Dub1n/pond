# R5.1 — Open H-cradle basket seat

## Decision status

**R5.1 is the selected basket-seat architecture and now has an issued
prototype hardware detail. It is not an accepted batch fabrication design and
does not validate the main rail or ring, its suspension or the deck.**

R5.1 retains a single-spine seat interface, initially illustrated on the
suspended ring described in [R3-rail.md](R3-rail.md), but replaces R3's two
independently bound crossbars and R5's two basket-sized square plates with an
**open, mechanically captured basket cradle**. In plan, the upper member is
broadly H-shaped: two transverse bearing arms support the basket's reinforced
base bands and a smaller central region connects them over the spine. Two
compact lower bridges capture the rail underside. Four sleeved bolts join the
upper and lower parts beside the rail without drilling or squeezing the hollow
main spine. The prototype movement stop uses two no-hole polyester cam straps
per seat; a single through-bushed rail pin is retained only as the positive
fallback if the strap stop slips in testing.

The fixed design decision is this open upper member, two lower bridges and
four sleeved bolts arranged as two separated capture stations. Earlier R5.1
development deliberately left rail size open; the integrated prototype now
selects 38 x 38 x 5 mm rail and the matching transverse hardware below. The
separation of the two along-rail stations still comes from the measured basket
bearing bands. A later rail-size change would require a revised seat drawing
and local checks, though it would not change the R5.1 architecture.

This document develops the local basket seat only. The predecessor
[R5 — Plates](R5-plates.md) remains the record of the full-square-plate
iteration. Its YAML and renders illustrate the inherited ring and the original
four-bolt capture principle, but they do **not** show the R5.1 plate outline,
bolt positions or lower bridges. Produce a separate editable model after the
actual baskets have been measured and the prototype dimensions below have
passed their local tests.

## Separation of responsibilities

Separating the basket seat from the rest of the carrier is useful because each
part has a different job and different failure modes. It does not mean that
the parts are structurally independent. Loads and movement cross every
interface:

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    B[Basket and planting assembly] -->|gravity, uplift, pitch and yaw| S[R5.1 basket seat]
    S -->|vertical load, torque and side load| R[GRP main ring]
    R -->|distributed reactions| H[Rail suspension]
    H -->|vertical and horizontal reactions| D[Joists, deck frame and foundations]
```

For this iteration:

| Area | Owns | Does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basket and planting assembly | Basket-base strength, reinforced bearing bands, removable cartridge, media retention, crown depth, fish guard, soaked mass and a sound feature for retention | Seat, rail or deck capacity |
| **R5.1 basket seat** | Basket bearing, local pitch/yaw/uplift restraint, seat-to-spine fit, local plate/bolt/sleeve behaviour, movement along the spine, drainage and basket removal | Whole-ring torsion, ring-corner strength or deck capacity |
| GRP main ring | Spine bending and torsion, ring racking, corners, cut-face sealing, flooding and drainage | Suspension or supporting-deck capacity |
| Rail suspension | Ring position, support sharing, horizontal restraint, cord or hanger durability, support-loss behaviour and future replacement | Joist, beam, connector or foundation spare capacity |
| Deck and foundations | Joists, hangers, beams, bracing, slabs, ground and the approved vertical/horizontal load envelope | Adequacy of the basket or its seat |

The boundaries are an engineering aid, not a restriction on later design. A
future bracket could combine seat and suspension functions; a new carrier
could replace the single spine; or a basket could incorporate its own cradle.
Any such proposal must redraw the boundaries and check the new combined load
paths rather than inheriting acceptance from R5.1.

## What R5.1 is intended to improve

Relative to R3's bound crossbars, R5.1 is intended to:

- hold the two basket-bearing regions in one repeatable geometry;
- replace the two crossbar-to-spine cord bindings with mechanical transverse,
  yaw and uplift restraint;
- avoid drilling or locally clamping the spine for the four main capture
  bolts, while making the normal longitudinal stop a no-hole strap detail;
- transfer eccentric basket load through an inspectable upper/lower capture;
- make the complete seat removable without opening a ring corner; and
- provide a route to mechanical basket retention with fewer submerged knots.

Relative to R5's full square plates, R5.1 is intended to:

- remove plate that lies outside the necessary bearing and connection paths;
- replace the full lower square with two small bridges used only when uplift or
  overturning engages the rail underside;
- leave most of the basket base open for water exchange and cleaning;
- reduce drained weight, submerged drag, sediment shelves and material cost;
- shorten the length of rail hidden by the seat so supports have more possible
  locations; and
- keep drainage openings away from highly loaded plate regions by creating
  open space rather than drilling a nominal pattern of holes.

These are design intentions to prove in the prototype. Removing material can
increase plate bending and stress around internal corners, so a lighter outline
is not automatically a stronger or acceptable one.

## Proposed arrangement

### Coordinate convention

At one straight side of the ring:

- the **rail direction** runs along the 2,100 mm side;
- the **transverse direction** runs from beneath the deck toward the pond;
- the two basket bearing bands are separated along the rail direction; and
- each bolt station contains one bolt on each transverse side of the spine.

### Upper H member

The upper structural-GRP member comprises:

1. two transverse bearing arms beneath the basket's measured reinforced base
   bands;
2. a central connecting region over the spine; and
3. sufficient local material around the four bolt holes for the selected
   washers, edge distances and load spreading.

The arms need cover only the basket areas capable of carrying load. Their
width, projection and spacing come from the real B23 and B28 bases rather than
their nominal rim sizes. Widen a bearing arm or add a local pad where the
basket needs more load distribution; do not restore a full square by default.

Use generous radii where each bearing arm meets the central region. Sharp
re-entrant corners create stress concentrations and are not acceptable in a
cut GRP H shape. Round, deburr and resin-seal the outside perimeter and every
hole.

A monolithic flat H is the baseline geometry to investigate. A hybrid H, using
two short structural-GRP tubes or bars as the bearing arms and a compact flat
central saddle, is an allowed comparison. The hybrid may provide more bending
stiffness for less material, but it reintroduces arm-to-saddle connections that
need their own broad-bearing detail.

### Lower bridges

Do not use a basket-sized lower plate. Provide two separate lower bridges, one
at each bolt station. Each bridge crosses beneath the spine and joins that
station's two bolts.

The bridges are normally unloaded by gravity. They engage the rail underside
when basket uplift or overturning tries to lift one side of the upper member.
Size them for that reaction, bolt and washer bearing, handling impact and the
agreed connection safety factor. Their outline should expose the remaining
rail underside to water and inspection.

### Bolts and spacer sleeves

Retain four through-bolts as two separated pairs along the rail. One pair alone
would permit much easier yaw and would give poor redundancy. Use M8 A4/316
hardware for the issued prototype. The actual stack and supplier guidance
still control final bolt length and tightening torque.

Each bolt passes through the upper member, a smooth rigid sleeve and its lower
bridge. The sleeves:

- set the clear distance between upper and lower parts;
- prevent bolt tightening from pinching the hollow rail;
- sit immediately outside the two rail faces and act as transverse keepers;
  and
- provide a repeatable small running clearance around the actual tube.

The rail is selected as 38 x 38 x 5 mm for this prototype. Use four smooth,
unfilled POM-C acetal sleeves, **16 mm outside diameter, 8.2-8.5 mm bore and
39 mm long**, with centres 28 mm either side of the rail centreline. Their
inner tangents are 1 mm clear of the nominal rail faces. Make them from solid
rod with square ends and a lightly chamfered bore. Measure the purchased tube
and make all four sleeves as one matched set; adjust sleeve length, not bolt
torque, if the tube is not exactly 38 mm high.

The sleeve is mainly a compression spacer, but it also takes local transverse
bearing when it acts as a rail-face keeper. POM-C is selected provisionally for
its stiffness, machinability, low moisture uptake and low cost. Do not replace
it with flexible pneumatic tube, thin-wall hose, nylon or an unidentified
plastic. Increasing the sleeve outside diameter is unnecessary at the listed
prototype reactions and would require moving the bolt axes outward to retain
rail clearance.

Use an 8 mm upper H and two lower bridges, each **50 mm along rail x 110 mm
transverse x 8 mm thick**, cut from the same accepted balanced structural-GRP
sheet. Put each bridge's two M8 axes on its transverse centreline at +/-28 mm.
Round its corners to at least 10 mm radius. The corner plates use a separate
9.5 mm flat bar because their governing action is joint stiffness, not local seat
flexure; see [seat-plate-study.md](../../calcs/seat-plate-study.md).

Use two transverse bearing arms, each 50 mm wide in the rail direction, joined
by a longitudinal central strip **90 mm wide in the transverse direction**
above the rail. Radius the four internal H corners at least 25 mm. Put the two
rail-capture stations on the bearing-arm centrelines.
The **only dimensions still transferred from the real basket** are the centre
spacing of its two sound base-bearing bands and the required arm projection.
Make each arm 20 mm longer than the measured sound base width, leaving 10 mm
support beyond it at both ends. Trace those two features directly to the
cutting template; do not infer them from the nominal 23 or 27 cm rim.

Fit the sleeves to a sample of the purchased rail. The former 1 mm side and
underside clearances are starting trial values only. Zero clearance can jam or
preload the rail; excessive clearance permits rocking and impact. Include tube
tolerance, sealed cut surfaces, grit and expected biological growth when
setting the accepted fit.

Place the two bolt stations as close as practical to the two basket bearing
regions so the load path through the upper member is short. Their final
positions must also provide:

- supplier-approved hole and plate-edge distances;
- full washer bearing on sound laminate;
- clearance from the actual tapered basket throughout removal travel;
- clearance from rail supports and ring-corner plates; and
- access to hold both ends of every fastener during assembly and renewal.

Use four M8 x 90 mm A4-80 partially threaded hex bolts, eight 24 mm
outside-diameter A4 broad washers, four A4 all-metal prevailing-torque nuts and
four smooth caps per seat. Put bolt heads and one washer on top and the nuts
below the lower bridges. Do not countersink structural GRP. The smooth shank
must cross both plates; change the whole batch to the next standard length if
the measured grip requires it. Tighten only to the seating torque established
by a wet connection coupon. Generic stainless-bolt torque is not acceptable:
long-term acetal compression, sleeve-end and washer bearing, not bolt tensile
resistance, govern the preload limit. The prevailing-torque nut retains the
assembly; do not rely on high bolt preload.

## Load paths

### Normal gravity

The basket's reinforced base bands bear on the two upper arms. The arms and
central region transfer that load by direct GRP bearing into the flat top of
the spine. Bolts, sleeves and lower bridges are not credited with carrying the
normal centred gravity load.

### Eccentric gravity and pitch

When the basket load moves across the spine, the upper member bears toward one
top rail edge and the opposite lower bridge can engage the rail underside. The
bolts join those two reactions into one cradle. Using the existing provisional
100 N basket load at 50 mm eccentricity, a rigid-body screen gives roughly
182 N at the loaded top edge and 82 N at the opposite underside. These are
useful prototype reactions, not final design loads or plate capacities.

### Yaw and transverse movement

The four sleeves straddle the spine at two stations. Contact between a sleeve
and rail face limits transverse movement; the separation between stations
limits yaw. The detail remains intentionally loose enough to avoid clamping
the spine, so measure the resulting movement rather than claiming zero play.

### Uplift

A basket-retention feature transfers uplift to the upper member. The bolts
then transfer it to both lower bridges, which bear on the rail underside. Test
handling uplift and an uneven one-corner pull; a centred upward test alone does
not exercise the governing local plate and bolt paths.

### Movement along the rail

The sleeved yokes can slide along a smooth spine. The prototype default is now
a **no-hole strap stop**: two independent 25 mm polyester endless cam straps per
seat, one beside each capture station. They are cheap enough to test in quantity,
can be installed by one person and avoid perforating the rail.

Cut two rounded strap slots in the 90 mm central strip of the upper H, one just
inside each capture station and outside the basket washer footprints. Each slot
is 30 x 7 mm, with a minimum 12 mm ligament to every plate edge, bolt washer
or basket-retention washer. Round the long slot ends to at least 3.5 mm radius
and seal the cut GRP. Route each strap down through the first slot, under the
38 mm rail, up through the matching slot and through its cam buckle. The strap
therefore clamps the upper H to the rail without passing over the basket base
or adding any rail hole.

Use smooth chafe sleeves wherever the webbing turns around GRP. Put the buckle
on the accessible deck side, not under the basket, and trim/hem the tail only
after the wet proof test. Mark the webbing at the buckle and mark the seat on
the rail with witness paint.

For the prototype, use either:

- a low-cost 25 mm high-tenacity polyester endless cam-buckle strap for trial
  fit and slip testing; or
- a marine 316-stainless cam-buckle endless strap if the whole buckle, spring
  and pin are confirmed 316 and the extra batch cost is acceptable.

Do not accept a zinc-plated buckle, unknown spring, hook-ended strap, ratchet
strap, elastic bungee or hook-and-loop strap for permanent pond service. The
strap is a movement stop only; it is not credited with basket gravity, handling
uplift or global suspension restraint.

Proof each installed strap pair after soaking. Pull the seat 0.10 kN along the
rail in both directions for five cycles, hold 0.10 kN for ten minutes each way,
then leave it wet for seven days and repeat. Accept the strap stop only if the
total permanent seat migration is at most 1 mm, the witness mark movement at
each buckle is at most 1 mm, and there is no webbing cut, buckle slip, GRP
whitening, basket snagging or retained twist in the H. If any sample fails,
do not add more wraps: use the positive fallback below.

The fallback, used only after a failed strap test or a direct decision to drill
the rail, is one through-bushed transverse stop pin with one elongated 3 mm
316L stop plate on the accessible face. Put the stop-pin axis at the seat's
longitudinal centre, midway between its two bearing arms and at rail mid-height.
Ream both vertical rail walls for one **8 mm-OD, 6.2 mm close-bore A4 bush**,
cut to the measured 38 mm rail width. Fit one M6 x 100 mm A4-80 partially
threaded bolt through washer, stop plate, spacer, bushed rail, washer, all-metal
nut and smooth cap.

Let `s` be the measured centre spacing of the two seat capture stations and
let the actual vertical sleeve outside diameter be `d` (nominally 16 mm). Cut
the stop plate:

```text
length along rail = s - d - 1 mm       (nominally s - 17 mm)
height = 25 mm
thickness = 3 mm
central pin hole = 6.5 mm
end radius = at least 8 mm; all other edges rounded at least 2 mm
```

Centre the plate on the stop pin so its two ends sit between the inner tangents
of that face's two vertical seat sleeves, with 0.5 mm nominal running clearance
at each end. Use one measured A4/316 spacer between the rail face and the inner
face of the stop plate so the plate clears the measured sleeves transversely by
0.5 mm without spreading the seat.

The one 8 mm fallback bush station removes about **80 mm², or 12.1%**, of the
660 mm² gross tube area at that local section. A simple gross-section screen
gives about **0.35% loss of vertical bending second moment** because the holes
are at mid-height, but about **17.9% local loss for horizontal in-plane
bending**. It also interrupts the closed-section torsional shear path; the bush
may be credited for bearing and clamp transfer, not for restoring that shear
path. Keep every fallback hole clear of corner plates and support-wrap zones,
use at least 32 mm clear edge-to-edge separation from any other rail hole unless
the GRP supplier requires more, and accept the fallback only after wet
horizontal, torsion and open-hole coupon tests.

## Permanent basket and removable planted cartridge

[plants-v2.md](plants-v2.md) makes the rigid B23/B28 outer basket the durable
structural part that stays on R5.1. The planted PB18/PB25 bag, its under-bag
harness and fish guard form a separate removable cartridge. None of those soft
parts retains the rigid basket or carries seat load.

Normal gravity bears through the measured basket base bands directly onto the
two H arms. Retain the empty rigid basket at four points using one smooth
**40 mm OD x 1.5 mm A4/316 penny washer inside the basket at each point**. Use
four M6 x 40 mm A4 ISO 7380-1 button-head screws, with the button head and
40 mm washer inside the basket, through the basket and 8 mm H arm, then an
18 mm OD A4 washer, all-metal locknut and smooth cap below the H. A typical
button head is about 10.5 mm diameter x 3.3 mm high, so the nominal internal
projection is only about 4.8 mm including the penny washer.

Choose each fixing axis at a measured sound base-rib intersection aligned over
an H arm. The complete 40 mm washer footprint must bear on at least four sound,
continuous ribs or strands and must not bridge a broken edge, distorted cell
or unsupported single strand. Use an existing opening only when that complete
bearing condition is met; otherwise make one clean hole by the method accepted
on the wet sample. Tighten only until the basket is captive and the washer is
seated; do not dish the washer or crush/flatten the plastic mesh.

Washer-only retention is the selected low-depth prototype, but the basket
supplier publishes no structural or immersed-creep capacity for its fine mesh.
Buy and drill one B23 and one B28 first. Reject the detail if four qualifying
intersections cannot align with the H arms, the mesh whitens or creeps, a
washer rocks/dishes, or the head/washer snags the wet bag. In that event do not
substitute a smaller washer: issue a measured thin continuous backing plate or
5 mm HDPE bar after testing. Use M6 x 50 mm only where a tested depth component
increases the measured stack; do not pack a 40 mm bolt with loose washers.
Record each accepted axis, washer footprint, height and clearance to the filled
PB18/PB25 footprint on the cutting template.

For normal planting service, leave the basket bolted to R5.1 and lift only the
cartridge with its two harness loops. For outer-basket replacement, remove the
cartridge, undo the four basket nuts, lift the four internal washers and
release the empty basket. The R5.1 seat can subsequently be removed by its
four M8 capture bolts without opening the ring.

The fish guard stays on the removable cartridge. It must not be laced to the
rigid basket, because mature growth may require the inexpensive guard panel to
be cut away and replaced during cartridge removal.

### Depth variants

The normal B23 and B28 seats keep their rigid basket rim just submerged at the
minimum normal water level. The *Butomus* PB25/B28 requires the **complete
basket and cartridge** to be lowered until the crown is 100–150 mm below
water; crowfoot needs an R5.1-compatible B23 downstand putting its rooting
point 200–350 mm below water. These are not internal-pot substitutions.

Use the same four-point broad-washer basket connection on the lower component of
any depth adaptor. Do not order or cut adaptor drops until the planted wet
prototype establishes the crown/rooting-point offset within its basket. Issue
that measured vertical offset directly on the basket-position sheet; no plant
load may hang from a bag seam, guard or harness.

Set the top of the accepted bearing arms at the required basket bearing datum.
Derive the main-spine elevation from the actual upper-member thickness and any
deliberate local bearing pad. Do not reuse either R3's 25 mm crossbar offset or
R5's 6 mm plate offset after changing the section.

## Issues owned by the R5.1 seat

The local design remains incomplete until the following are resolved:

1. **Actual basket interface.** Measure base dimensions, reinforced bands,
   taper, deformation, retention features and removal travel for B23 and B28.
2. **Upper-member capacity.** Verify the purchased 8 mm balanced plate and its
   fibre orientation; check both in-plane directions, arm bending, central-
   region bending, internal-corner stress, holes and wet long-term creep.
3. **Lower-bridge and connection capacity.** Check bridge flexure, bolt
   tension/shear, washer and sleeve bearing, edge distance, through-thickness
   laminate stress, acetal creep, seating torque and one loosened fastener.
4. **Fit over service life.** Establish an assembly clearance that neither
   rocks excessively when clean nor jams after soaking, grit and biofouling.
5. **Longitudinal restraint.** Prove the two no-hole strap stops in both
   directions after soaking and seven-day wet dwell. If they slip, prove the
   fallback through-bushed stop pin and single accessible-face stop plate.
6. **Basket retention.** Prove the four internal penny washers without mesh
   whitening, creep, bag damage or loss of independent cartridge removal.
7. **Water and maintenance.** Demonstrate open flow around the basket base,
   drainage, access for cleaning and no root or debris trap.
8. **Seat envelope.** Fit every cradle among the fixed lower cord wraps and
   locating collars, stop plates, ring corners, neighbouring baskets and
   removal routes. No upper H, basket or depth adaptor may touch a support
   cord, chafe sleeve, collar or corner cord post.
9. **Fish-facing finish.** Round and seal all laminate, sleeves, bridges,
   washers, heads, nuts and threads; leave no crevice or edge that can trap a
   fish, damage the liner or shed fibres.

The original R5 B28 station nearly filled the J3-to-J4 support interval before
the omitted support-wrap collars were included. R5.1 must reduce the length of
rail covered by its central region as well as removing its outer plate area.
An open H whose central web still occupies the former 280 mm length would
improve water flow and weight but would not solve that support-placement
conflict.

## Issues retained by the main ring

The following remain failures or hold points for the overall carrier, but they
are not evidence that one local basket seat is worse than another:

- torsion of the selected single spine under seat torque;
- in-plane ring racking and the bolted mitred-corner response;
- main-member bending, handling strength and support-loss spans;
- selected-profile resin, section tolerances, wet durability and directional
  properties;
- deliberate venting and drainage of every hollow ring side; and
- interaction between seat reactions, corner zones and the full basket layout.

R5.1 must report its vertical loads, horizontal loads and torques to the ring.
The ring must then carry those actions. A well-performing seat cannot validate
the ring, but neither should an unresolved ring question obscure the local
seat result.

## Issues retained by suspension and deck

R5.1 does not resolve:

- equilibrium and whole-ring roll under inclined supports;
- horizontal reactions created by real support angles;
- uneven load sharing, creep, chafe and failure of one support;
- post-deck access and replacement of every upper termination;
- capacity of the joists, hangers, straps, screws, beams and bracing;
- outer-beam uplift, slab/ground bearing or combined deck loading; or
- the five TBC limits in the retained load envelope in
  [design-C.md](design-C.md).

These questions apply to the connected single-spine ring with R5.1. Recalculate
the permanent load using the measured seat, basket and cartridge masses, but do
not require the local seat document to solve the supporting structure.

R4 remains useful evidence that improving a basket's local bearing does not by
itself establish global equilibrium. Its independent same-bank module lacks an
opposing horizontal reaction. R5.1 does not inherit that particular mechanism
because it remains on the connected R3 ring, whose opposing sides can provide
a complete reaction system if its corners and suspension pass their own tests.

## Prototype sequence

### Stage 1 — measured geometry

1. Buy and measure one B23 and one B28, including loaded base deformation,
   reinforced bands, taper, sound retention zones and removal envelope.
2. Mark the actual rail-support and corner exclusion zones on one full-size
   2,100 mm side. Trace every direct lower wrap, locating collar and corner cord
   post, and keep the complete seat/basket envelope outside those zones.
3. Transfer the two measured bearing bands to the specified 50 mm arms, 90 mm
   central strip and 25 mm internal radii. Record plate orientation and every
   remaining ligament before cutting.
4. Position the two strap slots beside the capture stations, clear of every
   sleeve washer and basket washer footprint. Mark the four basket penny washers
   at qualifying rib intersections on the purchased samples. Record any fallback
   stop-pin coordinate only if the strap test fails.

### Stage 2 — connection coupon

Build one representative sleeved-bolt and lower-bridge connection in the
selected materials. Tighten it using a documented procedure. Inspect for local
indentation, laminate whitening, splitting, washer tilt and permanent set.
Repeat after soaking and after one disassembly/reassembly. Do not infer this
behaviour from bolt tensile capacity.

Also immerse one representative acetal sleeve under 0.30 kN axial compression
for seven days. The 16/8.5 mm annulus then sees about 2.1 MPa, above the listed
seat reactions but still only a material screen. Reject splitting, ovalisation,
more than 0.2 mm permanent shortening, or any clearance change that causes
binding or unacceptable rocking.

### Stage 3 — one complete seat

Test one complete B28 cradle over dry ground and then after soaking:

- measured fully soaked out-of-water gravity load;
- 50 mm eccentric load and a deliberately localized bearing-band load;
- pitch, yaw and transverse movement;
- centred and one-corner handling uplift;
- alternating longitudinal force against the selected stop;
- wet strap-stop creep and buckle witness-mark movement;
- one deliberately loosened fastener;
- repeated planted-cartridge removal while the rigid basket stays attached;
- one empty rigid-basket removal and reinstallation;
- wet creep, whitening and snag checks at all four basket washers; and
- drainage, cleaning access and inspection after disassembly.

Use about 2 mm initial loaded movement as the existing prototype target, but
define where it is measured and distinguish plate flexure, connection play,
rail rotation and suspension movement.

### Stage 4 — representative full side

Install four representative stations on the same supported 2,100 mm ring side:
a shallow B23, shallow B28, *Butomus* B28 and crowfoot B23/downstand. Include
the six J2--J7 gravity loops, one opposed crossed pair, one two-legged C2
corner sling, all lower collars/seat stops, one complete corner and the finished
deck-edge mock-up. Use the final cartridge, guard and lifting harness at each
relevant station.

Record seat movement separately from spine twist, ring movement and suspension
movement. This validates the interfaces and removal sequence; it does not
accept the whole carrier.

## Acceptance for further development

Advance R5.1 from local prototype to the full-ring candidate only when:

- the measured basket loads bear on defined reinforced regions;
- all local stresses and deformations are checked using documented wet-service
  plate properties and appropriate connection factors;
- no upper arm, bridge, hole, sleeve or washer shows cracking, crushing,
  whitening, elongation or permanent set;
- the seat cannot detach, yaw, migrate or tip into the swimming route under the
  agreed gravity, eccentric, uplift and handling cases;
- the basket remains independently removable beneath the finished deck edge;
- the seat leaves useful water exchange and can be cleaned and inspected;
- its complete envelope fits the issued basket/support/corner layout; and
- its measured mass and reactions are issued to the ring, suspension and deck
  checks.

If R5.1 passes locally but the spine or ring rolls, racks or overloads its
supports, record the seat as successful and redesign the failing carrier area.
Do not reject a sound local cradle merely to preserve a linear revision path.
Equally, do not use a successful local test as evidence that the shared ring
and deck have passed.
