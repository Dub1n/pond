# Planting and basket schedule — v2

This is the working procurement, planting and maintenance specification for the
planted elements in Design C. It supersedes the internal-net-pot, loose outer
LECA fill, permanently laced basket lid, R3 crossbar and four lower basket-tie
instructions in [plants.md](plants.md). Keep `plants.md` as the earlier design
record; this version is the active planting specification.

The thirteen rail planters use a **removable planted cartridge inside a rigid
structural basket**. Install the carrier, R5.1 seats and empty rigid baskets
first. Prepare, soak and test the planted cartridges separately, then lower
them into the retained baskets. A cartridge can later be recovered without
removing its rigid basket or disturbing the rail.

[R5.1.md](R5.1.md) remains the selected basket-seat architecture for survey
and prototype, not an accepted batch fabrication design. It now specifies the
broad-washer basket retainer, through-bushed stop interface and depth-variant hold
points. Buy one B23 and one B28 attachment set first; buy the remaining seat
hardware only after the measured fit, wet creep and removal tests pass. The
lily, hornwort cage and willow-moss supports remain independent of the rail.

## Design intent

The composition keeps one connected, dark open-water centre while planting the
perimeter in layers. Two hard-rush clumps provide repeated tall,
winter-green corners; the single white *Butomus* is an offset summer accent;
low flowering and grass-like marginals soften the side edges; and one
controlled lily supplies the surface layer.

Preserve at least **65–70% of the plan** as one connected central swimming
area, approximately **70–80% of the surface** as open water and approximately
**80–90% of the floor** as unobstructed cleaning and swimming space. Harvest
watercress, mint, crowfoot and hornwort and remove the cut material from the
pond. Keep lily pads to about 20–30% of the surface.

The fabric planting bag is the controlled root and growing-medium boundary.
The rigid outer basket is the durable structural interface with R5.1, protects
the cartridge and transfers its load into the seat. The cartridge guard keeps
koi out of the medium. None of the planting bag, lifting harness, guard,
foliage or growing medium receives structural credit for retaining the rigid
basket on its seat.

### PB25 fit and alteration

Buy and test one B28 and one PB25 before placing the batch order. Fill, soak
and cap the bag, then check its entire insertion and lift path rather than only
the basket rim. Aim for at least **10 mm actual free clearance per side** at
the tightest section while the bag is wet, filled and slightly tilted.

If the bag wedges or has uncontrolled folds, form matched tapered seams on all
four sides, or use another symmetrical alteration that reduces both plan
dimensions, until the filled footprint follows the measured basket taper.
Record the accepted finished length and width rather than assuming the result
remains square. A footprint around 21–22 cm in both directions and 18–19 cm
deep would retain roughly 8–9 L gross volume while providing much better
running clearance.
Use pond-safe rot-resistant polyester thread, multiple rows of stitching and
no metal staples or exposed sharp ends. Keep altered seams outside the main
root ball where practicable. The lifting harness described below must carry
the recovery load; do not rely on an original or altered bag seam as a lifting
connection.

## Planting-cartridge construction

### Layer arrangement

Each rail cartridge contains, from bottom to top:

1. a PB18 or PB25 fine-woven planting bag, altered only after the fit trial;
2. a full-plan root zone of pre-wetted aquatic loam or an accepted
   loam-rich/LECA prototype mix;
3. the plant root ball or divided rooted portion at its specified crown level;
4. a **20–30 mm** washed, lime-free 4–10 mm gravel cap;
5. a smooth 5–6 mm HDPE koi guard appropriate to the plant's growth habit; and
6. a broad lifting harness supporting the bag from below and providing two
   accessible recovery loops.

The planted volume extends across the whole bag. There is no 150 mm internal
net pot and no separate unused LECA chamber in the standard cartridge.

### Growing-medium prototype

Do not set a universal mix from catalogue bulk densities and do not aim for a
barely neutral cartridge. LECA porosity, retained air, saturation time and
segregation make calculated loose-volume buoyancy unreliable. Dense loam or
gravel can settle while LECA rises during filling and handling.

For the first B23 and B28, compare by loose volume:

- 100% aquatic loam;
- 75% aquatic loam and 25% fully pre-soaked LECA; and
- 50% aquatic loam and 50% fully pre-soaked LECA.

Use the lightest blend that remains cohesive, retains the plant securely,
does not segregate or release fines and passes both submerged and drained-load
tests. The 75:25 mix is the preferred first lightweight trial, not an accepted
batch recipe. Watercress may use a somewhat more open, gravel-rich loam mix
only after a growth and anchorage trial; pure LECA/gravel is not the baseline.
Crowfoot uses a soft loam-rich rooting bed. Hard rush and *Butomus* require the
strongest anchorage and may retain a higher loam proportion.

Test the **complete installed assembly**, not a loose media sample. Record the
cartridge-only wet mass for lifting and handling, then separately record the
fully flooded rigid-basket-plus-cartridge assembly for underwater stability
and the drained rail/deck load. A litre of trapped air can provide
approximately 1 kg of uplift. After releasing all air, retain the existing
provisional installed-assembly targets:

- PB18/B23: **0.5–1.5 kg apparent downward weight underwater**; and
- tall PB25/B28: test whether **2–3 kg apparent downward** is needed to resist
  rocking.

Add washed gravel in approximately 250 g increments only when required. Keep
it within the defined cap or rooting mix: if meeting the stability target
would make the cap deeper than 30 mm, revise the mix or provide a tested
cartridge keeper instead of piling gravel higher. Do not distribute a
purchased ballast quantity by rule. Record the fully soaked out-of-water mass
of both the cartridge and complete rigid-basket assembly because the latter
governs the rail and deck reaction when the pond is drained. The earlier
100 N completed-basket value remains only a comparison screen.

### Lifting harness

Do not recover a planted bag by its fabric rim, seams, foliage or koi guard;
the bag supplier publishes no lifting capacity. Provide two broad,
pond-compatible, low-stretch polyester straps crossing beneath the bag and
rising outside it to two accessible loops. Join or constrain the straps so
they cannot slide together and tip the cartridge. Keep every cut end smooth,
sealed and outside fish contact.

The harness remains with the cartridge. In normal service the bag bears on
the rigid basket floor; the harness is for controlled insertion and recovery,
not a hanging planter. Select the final strap width, stitching and capacity
from the measured wet cartridge mass and test at least the agreed handling
factor over dry ground.

### Koi guard and plant growth

A fine guard permanently laced to the rigid basket would defeat cartridge
removal. A reusable basket-hinged guard is also not universally workable:
after a season, hairgrass, watercress, forget-me-not, crowfoot and other shoots
may grow through it and prevent it opening independently.

Treat the guard as part of the **removable planted cartridge**, not part of the
permanent rigid basket:

- support and attach it to the cartridge's upper harness belt or a light,
  smooth-edged pond-safe HDPE perimeter frame, not to weak bag fabric alone;
- provide a cord hinge or flexible lacing along one edge and accessible
  reusable clips on the opposite edge for initial planting and dormant-season
  inspection;
- cut one or more smooth planting windows sized for the crown or intended
  emergence zone rather than forcing all shoots through 5–6 mm apertures;
- keep the gravel cap beneath the guard; and
- lift the guard, harness, bag, medium and plant together as one cartridge
  whenever mature growth prevents the guard opening.

No fine koi guard can remain completely unentangled by a mature spreading
plant indefinitely. The cord hinge is for planting and early or dormant-season
access; do not expect it to open independently once a spreading plant has
grown through the panel. At division or bag replacement, lift the complete
cartridge, cut back the plant and, where necessary, cut away and replace the
inexpensive guard panel. The smooth HDPE perimeter frame and lifting harness
should remain reusable after inspection. A two-piece overlapping guard around
a discrete crown is preferable for hard rush, *Butomus*, pennyroyal and marsh
marigold. Use a larger deliberate emergence window and regard the panel as a
replaceable service item for hairgrass, watercress, forget-me-not and
crowfoot. Round every cut mesh and frame edge and leave no exposed metal or
projecting fastening in the fish-accessible zone.

The guard must not carry the wet cartridge, act as the basket's structural
uplift retainer or bridge an unsupported bag. Add an accessible positive
cartridge keeper only if the completed flooded cartridge can lift or tip
inside the outer basket; prove that keeper separately from the broad-washer
R5.1 basket-to-seat connection.

## Prototype and installation sequence

1. Buy one B23, one B28, one PB18 when available and one PB25.
2. Measure each rigid basket's top and minimum internal dimensions, base,
   taper, reinforced bearing bands and possible basket-retention features.
3. Dry-fit the empty bags. Make a trial harness and guard frame for each size.
4. Prepare the candidate media with fully pre-soaked LECA and washed gravel.
5. Fill, plant and cap the cartridges. Fold the PB18 top inward within B23.
   Test PB25 unaltered, then sew in its width only if required by the measured
   wet fit.
6. Soak each complete cartridge and work out all trapped air. Record the
   cartridge-only wet handling mass, then place it in its flooded rigid basket
   and record the complete assembly's apparent underwater mass initially and
   after 7 and 28 days. After a consistent drain period, record cartridge-only
   and complete-assembly out-of-water masses separately.
7. Insert and recover each cartridge for at least ten wet cycles. Reject any
   arrangement that jams, sheds soil, damages seams, traps gravel between bag
   and basket, deforms the outer basket or gives less than 10 mm working
   clearance at the tightest point.
8. Load-test the under-bag harness over dry ground. Repeat insertion and
   recovery beneath a full-size deck-edge mock-up, including the required
   pondward movement before lifting.
   With the cartridge removed, release the R5.1 basket retainer and also
   demonstrate that the empty rigid basket can move pondward and lift past the
   finished deck edge and all final seat hardware for basket replacement and
   seat inspection.
9. Finalise the root mix and production quantities only after the first
   PB18/B23 and PB25/B28 tests. Repeat the stability check with a representative
   tall hard-rush cartridge.
10. Install and accept the rail, R5.1 seats, depth adaptors and empty rigid
    baskets before adding planted cartridges. The *Butomus* and crowfoot
    depth components remain rail-dependent hold points.
11. Lower each pre-soaked cartridge using both harness loops, fit any cartridge
    keeper, and verify crown level, apparent weight, guard security and clear
    pondward recovery route.

Reject a cartridge if its mix segregates, its guard or bag seam takes lifting
load, it loses its downward reserve, fines escape, it cannot be recovered
without disturbing the rigid basket, or its drained mass exceeds the accepted
R5.1/rail/deck load envelope.

## Independent planted elements

- **L35 lily:** omit LECA unless a separate lily-specific trial justifies it.
  Fill with wet aquatic loam, retain the supplied rootball, keep the growing
  point exposed, and cap with washed gravel and a removable guard. Use a stable
  removable riser to change depth.
- **H70 hornwort cage:** fold and lace heavy HDPE mesh into a closed
  30 × 30 × 70 cm column with a removable top. Put smooth slate weights in
  four internal bottom-corner pockets and stand it on smooth inert 5–10 cm
  feet. Cable ties may assist assembly but are not the sole permanent closure.
- **M20 moss slates:** smooth every edge and tie the moss with pond-safe thread
  or cord. Do not bury it.

## Inspection and maintenance

- Weekly in the growing season: prevent watercress from joining into a
  continuous mat and remove loose or dead material.
- Seasonally: harvest excess watercress, mint, crowfoot and hornwort; remove
  dead *Butomus* foliage; keep the lily within its surface-area limit; inspect
  accessible cartridge loops and guard connections.
- Annually: during an appropriate plant-rest or cutback period, recover a
  representative PB18 and PB25 cartridge. Inspect bag fabric and seams, lifting
  straps, frame, guard, soil retention, roots, gravel cap, rigid basket,
  cartridge keeper and the separate R5.1 basket retainer.
- Divide a plant when its cartridge becomes congested. Expect the Caltha,
  watercress, mint, hairgrass and forget-me-not cartridges to need the closest
  attention.
- When replacing a planting bag, work ashore. Cut back or divide the plant,
  cut away any guard mesh captured by mature growth, transfer the retained
  plant to a new bag, and reuse the harness/frame only after inspection.
