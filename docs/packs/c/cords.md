# Pack C rail suspension — cords, fittings and cord map

## Selected arrangement

Use **44 independent 6 mm low-stretch polyester cords**:

- 24 gravity cords from straight joists J2–J7, six on each side;
- 4 inclined corner cords from non-projecting side eyes on the existing C2
  diagonal joists; and
- 16 crossed stabilisers, two opposite-handed pairs in the J2–J3 and J6–J7
  bays on every side.

The corner cords replace the former eight J1/J8 cords. Do not also fit those
end cords. Keep the crossed cords at the middle bays rather than moving them
to the corners. Each cord is a separately replaceable load path.

The selected termination is:

- a supplier-spliced lower eye around a 6 mm A4/316 thimble;
- an A4 load-rated captive shackle to a positively through-fastened articulated
  rail eye;
- a free upper end through one hard-anodised **Clamcleat CL253AN**, hung from a
  through-bolted joist cheek pin and rigged 1:1 to the maker's drawing; and
- one double-overhand stopper in the unloaded tail, at least 150 mm beyond the
  cleat, as a secondary catch.

This requires one simple repeatable stopper knot, tied after the cleat is
holding the load. No load-bearing knot is tied under tension and no second
person is needed to hold the rail while adjustment is made. The cam cleat is
above water and remains accessible below a removable deck board.

The CL253AN is selected because it is compact, works with 4–8 mm braided line,
contains its own roller and is intended for quick positive adjustment of
trapeze and vang loads. [Clamcleat does not publish a generic safe working
load](https://www.clamcleat.com/trapeze-vang-cleat.html), because holding
depends on the rope. Buy and test one cleat with the selected rope before the
batch; then proof every completed termination at 0.30 kN. If it slips, damages
the cover or cannot hold the wet bedding test, use the
[Spinlock PXR0206](https://www.spinlock.co.uk/en-GB/row/products/pxr-cam-cleat),
which has a published 140 kg load figure, and issue its mounting bracket before
installation. Do not mix cleat types on the finished ring.

## Calculation result and limits

The selected 44-cord rigid-ring screen uses 10 N seating tension and a
conservative effective wet/bedded `EA=6 kN`:

| Result                                                       |       Selected layout |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------: |
| Rigid-body constraint rank                                   |                6 of 6 |
| Uneven 1.80 kN case plus 5 N m: maximum cord                 |                75.8 N |
| Worst maximum with any one cord unavailable                  |                84.6 N |
| 95th-percentile maximum with +/-1.5 mm free-length variation |                99.5 N |
| Maximum screened upper downward / horizontal / resultant     | 91.7 / 45.9 / 102.5 N |
| 95th-percentile rigid-body movement                          |               3.42 mm |

All 44 individual-cord-unavailable cases found feasible equilibrium in this
screen. The figures are not deck capacities. The physical ring is flexible,
the corner moment/rotation curve is still a release gate, and the five deck
limits in [design-C.md](design-C.md) remain TBC. Use 0.30 kN only as the proof
load for one completed connection over dry ground.

## Rope and end preparation

Use one batch of 6 mm, 100% polyester, 16-plait-covered low-stretch rope with
a published minimum break load of at least 6 kN. The reference product is
[Marlow Excel Pro 6 mm](https://shop.marlowropes.com/excel-pro-6mm-grey-100m-r-es0230):
the maker states 100% polyester and an 887 kg minimum break load. A supplier
such as [Jimmy Green Marine](https://jimmygreen.com/product/marlow-6mm-excel-pro/)
can supply line by length and splice eyes; order a sample splice and written
finished-eye dimensions before the batch.

Do not use nylon, polypropylene, natural fibre, an unidentified accessory
cord, wire-rope ferrules on textile rope or wire-rope grips. Do not substitute
HMPE/Dyneema without rechecking creep, cleat grip and the modelled axial
stiffness.

The table below gives **eye-centre-to-cleat force-line lengths**, not rope cut
lengths. For the first sample, add the splice maker's stated bury/taper length,
the lower thimble allowance, 200 mm of adjustment tail and 150 mm beyond the
final cleat position. Transfer the accepted sample to a physical cutting jig;
do not calculate the remaining 43 cuts from a nominal product length.

Buy **one 100 m reel** for the installed batch. The 44 nominal force-line
lengths total 18.1 m, but splice buries, thimble turns, 350 mm of
adjustment/stopper tail per cord, supplier setup pieces, proof coupons and
same-batch replacements are controlled by the accepted sample rather than the
map. Do not reduce the order to a theoretical 60 m allowance until the splice
supplier has issued and totalled all 44 actual cut lengths. Heat-seal only the
cut end in accordance with the rope maker's instructions; do not leave a hard
sharp blob.

## Lower rail connection

At each unique straight-rail lower station fit one Wichard 6684 universal
folding pad eye or an equivalent forged 316L articulated eye with a published
all-direction working load. The 6684 is 40 mm square, has 23.4 mm hole centres,
two 7 mm fixing holes and a published 750 kg nautical working load. Mount it
centrally on the **top face** of the rail so the cord clears the tube and its
vertical component acts on the rail centreline without adding suspension-
eccentricity roll.

Put one 50 mm along-rail x 44 mm transverse x 3 mm 316L spreader plate between
the pad-eye base and rail, with 8 mm corner radii. Centre the pad eye at the
station and rail centreline. Orient its fixing axes along the rail, 11.7 mm
each side of the station. Use two M6 x 65 mm A4-80 countersunk bolts vertically
through the pad eye, spreader, both rail walls and a second 50 x 44 x 3 mm
backing plate below the rail. Ream each rail-wall hole for an **8 mm-OD,
6.2 mm close-bore A4 bush cut to the measured external rail height**, with its
ends flush against the two plates. Fit broad washers and all-metal nuts below;
change the whole batch bolt length if the sample stack lacks full engagement.
Cap both nuts. Do not use self-tappers, a U-bolt, a grub screw or clamp
friction.

The catalogue `C=15 mm` dimension gives the nominal eye/shackle tangent
`r=1050, Z=-207` after the 3 mm spreader. Confirm that point on the purchased
eye, shackle and thimble sample; if it differs by more than 3 mm, update the map
and rerun before cutting cords. The through-bush transmits tightening across
the hollow tube and presents a smooth 8 mm bearing surface to both rail walls;
the M6 screw is a clamp and keeper inside it.

The top-mounted eye is also a hard seat-exclusion zone. Before drilling, trace
the purchased pad eye, both shackles where fitted, thimbles and cords in their
folded, unloaded and loaded positions on the full-size basket-position sheet.
Keep every R5.1 H, basket and depth adaptor outside that swept outline with at
least 10 mm measured clearance. Do not solve a clash by rotating the eye off
the mapped force line or by letting the seat bear on the eye.

There are 24 unique straight lower stations. At J2, J3, J6 and J7 one gravity
cord and one crossed cord share the **fixed pad-eye ring**, not a moving rope
wrap. Give each cord its own load-rated M6 shackle and thimble, put them on
opposite sides of the folding ring and add a smooth separator if the two
thimbles can touch. If two shackles do not articulate independently on the
purchased sample, fit two eyes at least 30 mm apart and update the as-built
coordinate and cord length; do not force both cords into one shackle.

Use the lower corner-eye bridge in [corner.md](corner.md) at C1-D to C4-D.
Do not drill another eye through the mitred tube.

The selected eye line gives about 0.188 kN heavy-side outward action. Retain
the former 0.243 kN result as the conservative coordinate and assembly-error
sensitivity.

## Upper joist connection

### Straight joists J2–J7

At each of the 24 used straight joist tips fit a pair of **40 x 6 x 200 mm
A4/316 cheek plates**, centred on the 75 mm joist depth. Each blank runs from
180 mm behind the timber end to 20 mm pondward of it. Put two M8 timber-bolt
axes 60 and 130 mm behind the end. Put the M8 clevis-pin axis exactly in the
timber-end plane, leaving the stated 20 mm of cheek plate beyond it; the pin
does not pass through timber. Use three M8 x 90 mm A4-80 partially threaded
bolts per joist end: two timber bolts and one clevis bolt/pin. Fit 24 mm broad
washers, all-metal locknuts, smooth spacers and thread caps. The selected DIN
931 pattern should leave smooth shank through both cheeks and the 47 mm timber;
verify that condition and full nut engagement on the sample before ordering
the remaining 69 sets.

At the 0.30 kN connection proof, equal load sharing gives 0.15 kN per cheek and
9 N m at the first timber bolt. A simple 40 x 6 mm strip screen is about 38 MPa
bending before hole effects. The purchased plate, pin holes, timber bearing and
complete joist connection still have to pass the proof test and deck check.

Hang each CL253AN from the clevis pin using the maker's PT230SSE stainless eye
or a load-rated A4 shackle. J2, J3, J6 and J7 each carry two cleats; keep them
independent with smooth spacers and align each with its own lower eye. The pin
centre is the `U` coordinate. Do not screw an eye into the joist end grain.

Keep the two cheek through-bolts at the stated edge distances unless the
timber-connection check requires more. Record the actual hanger, inner
connection and outer support because the rail proof does not validate the
joist-to-ground path. Reject any assembly whose pin centre is more than 3 mm
from the mapped force line and rerun before cord cutting if the timber check
forces a change.

### Diagonal C2 joists

Use the four non-projecting side-eye assemblies in [corner.md](corner.md).
Each eye is 55 mm behind its joist tip and offset to the outward side face, so
its cord is inclined in both plan axes. These four supports must pass the
0.30 kN line proof and the timber/deck check must include the nominal
0.248 kN vertical and 0.168 kN horizontal proof components.

### Deck access

Every cleat, tail, pin, nut and witness mark must remain visible and removable
after decking. Mark a removable board/access strip before laying the boards.
A support hidden behind a permanent board is rejected even if it passes its
proof test.

## Crossed-cord separation

The two crossed cords in each bay cross at approximately:

```text
local J2-J3 bay: a=-629.875, r=1100, Z=-47.25
local J6-J7 bay: a=+629.875, r=1100, Z=-47.25
```

The crossing is not a joint. Fit a 100 mm-long smooth silicone or EPDM chafe
sleeve to each cord, then hold the two sleeves in separate planes at least
12 mm apart with a loose non-load-bearing HDPE separator clipped to one cord.
The separator must not shorten either line or prevent one cord being replaced.

## One-person installation and proof

1. Number every upper and lower eye from the map. Fit and proof the joist cheek
   assemblies while the framing is open.
2. Assemble and test the bare ring, lower eyes and corners over dry ground.
   Keep the seats and baskets off.
3. Have the 44 lower eyes spliced and labelled. Fit thimbles and captive
   shackles. Inspect every splice against the accepted sample.
4. Attach all lower shackles and hang the CL253AN cleats from their upper pins.
   Route crossed cords through their sleeves/separators without letting them
   touch.
5. Support the bare ring temporarily at its design level. Feed each free end
   through its cleat in the maker's 1:1 arrangement. Pull with a small spring
   scale until the standing part reaches 10 N, lock the cleat, mark the cord at
   the cleat and tie the tail stopper. One person can do this because the cleat
   holds the previous adjustment.
6. Work around opposite sides in a crossing sequence. Confirm each corner
   cord matches its mapped inclined force line, clears timber/deck by at least
   5 mm, the ring is level and both diagonals are equal. Re-adjust only from
   accessible cleats.
7. Apply 0.30 kN to each completed support over dry ground, one at a time, for
   five minutes. Record cleat movement, cord mark movement, splice bedding,
   rail-eye movement, timber indentation and nut witness marks. Reject any
   slip over 1 mm, cover damage, eye rotation into an edge or permanent set.
8. Soak the lower half and repeat 10 N adjustment and the full load cases after
   24 hours and seven days. Repeat with the calculated worst cord removed.
9. Only after the flexible-ring/corner and deck gates pass, install the empty
   R5.1 seats and baskets, repeat level/load checks, and close the removable
   deck access.

Inspect after one week, one month, the first drain-down and at least annually.
Replace a cord if the cover is glazed, cut, flattened, locally hard, abraded or
has moved relative to its witness mark. Keep the four spare cleats and labelled
same-batch rope dry and dark.
