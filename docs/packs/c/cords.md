# Pack C rail suspension - direct cord loops

## Selected prototype arrangement

Use **36 independent 6 mm low-stretch polyester cord items**:

- 24 gravity loops from straight joists J2-J7, six on each side;
- 8 crossed stabilising loops, one opposite-handed pair per side; and
- 4 corner slings, each wrapped around one C2 diagonal joist and terminating
  at two separated lower points on the adjacent corner legs.

Every straight and crossed support is one closed cord loop bearing directly
around its timber joist and the 38 x 38 mm GRP rail. Each loop has two nearly
parallel legs and is joined with an accepted permanent loop knot below an
accessible removable deck board. The rail is held on temporary level supports
while the knots are set.

Each corner sling is one continuous cord. Its midpoint bears around the C2
joist and its two free ends descend to smooth cord posts on the existing
`(55,0)` and `(0,55)` M8 corner-bolt axes. This puts one lower point on each
rail leg without weakening the rail or adding holes to the corner plates.

The loops, knots, chafe sleeves and stops are prototype details until they pass
the dry and wet tests below. The direct-loop method is the only active
suspension detail.

## Cord and knot

Use one batch of 6 mm, 100% polyester, covered low-stretch rope with a
published minimum break load of at least 6 kN. Marlow Excel Pro 6 mm remains a
suitable reference product. Do not use nylon, polypropylene, natural fibre,
unidentified accessory cord or HMPE without rechecking wet behaviour, creep,
knot security and stiffness.

The first support loop establishes the production knot and tail allowance. A
well-dressed double fisherman's bend with generous tails is a reasonable
prototype for compatible braided polyester, but the selected rope maker's
guidance and the proof test govern. Add a separate backup and witness-mark
both tails. Place the knot beside the joist where it remains accessible, not
under the rail or against a basket.

Make one full-size gravity loop, crossed loop and corner sling, including
wraps, knot tails and spares, then total the measured cutting schedule. Do not
buy a batch reel from theoretical straight-line lengths.

## Straight and crossed support loops

At each selected station:

1. Fit a replaceable smooth polyester tubular-webbing or similarly accepted
   chafe sleeve wherever the support cord turns around timber or GRP.
2. Pass one unspliced cord around the joist and beneath the rail so that two
   legs connect the members. Keep the two legs beside one another without
   crossing or trapping debris.
3. Hold the rail on a rigid temporary support at the required installed height.
   Pull the loop snug, dress and close its permanent loop knot beside the
   accessible joist face, add the backup and mark both tails.
4. Bind a tight replaceable 3-4 mm polyester locating collar immediately on
   each side of the lower wrap. The collars locate the support along the rail
   but carry none of its vertical load.
5. Fit the upper stop described below and mark the cord at both timber and rail
   so any walking is visible.

For a crossed support, the upper joist and lower rail station differ. The
selected logical routes are:

```text
each side: U2 -> L3 and U3 -> L2
```

The equivalent J6-J7 pair may be substituted on a complete side if the actual
basket layout gives materially better clearance, but use the same pair on all
four sides unless the recalculation explicitly accepts an asymmetric map.
Separate the two diagonal loops at their crossing with individual smooth
sleeves and a loose non-load-bearing separator. The crossing is not a knot or
structural node.

Do not cut V notches or grooves into the 5 mm GRP rail wall. Do not encapsulate
the load-bearing cord in epoxy. If the replaceable lashing collars walk during
testing, trial small rounded GRP locating blocks or raised resin beads bonded
to a lightly abraded and cleaned rail coupon. Their bond is positional only:
the complete support must remain vertically secure if a locator detaches.

## Upper joist wrap and stop

The cord wrap carries load by bearing around the joist. A small treated-timber
stop on the **upper face** prevents the wrap moving toward the pond-facing free
end; it is not a hanging eye and is not credited with carrying the vertical
reaction. Use two suitable stainless screws into sound side-grain, with pilot
holes, preserved cut surfaces and adequate distance from the joist end. Round
the stop edges facing the cord and keep the cord on its chafe sleeve.

For the first full-size template, put the wrap centreline **35 mm back from the
surveyed joist tip** and trial a full-width stop approximately 47 mm transverse
x 15 mm along-joist x 10 mm high, immediately pondward of the protected wrap.
Use two pilot-drilled A4 screws across the stop width. These are fit-up starting
dimensions only: confirm screw end distance, splitting, stop bearing and the
remaining thickness of the relieved 28 mm deck board before adopting them.

Set the exact wrap and stop positions from the surveyed joist, the real cord
and chafe sleeve, and a full-size removable-deck-board template. Prefer a wrap
close to the pond-facing end to limit horizontal cord force, but
leave enough sound upper face pondward of it for the stop and its screws. The
removable deck board must have smooth underside relief so it neither bears on
the stop or cord nor exposes them above the finished deck.

Record the effective force-line tangent of every accepted wrap in plan and
height. Rerun the suspension calculation before cutting the batch if the
straight loops, crossed loops or C2 slings differ from the prototype
coordinates. The stop is on top, but the two loaded cord legs normally leave
near the joist underside; record those tangents rather than using the stop
height as the force-line height. Nothing may project beyond a joist tip.

## Corner sling

Retain the stiff paired-plate corner in [corner.md](corner.md). On each corner,
use the existing M8 axes at local `(55,0)` and `(0,55)` as the two lower sling
points. Fit a short smooth metal compression spacer over each bolt above the
top corner plate, with a broad retaining washer above it. The bolt clamp stack
must remain metal-to-metal; the cord and any polymer chafe sleeve must not sit
in the corner-joint preload path. No thread may touch the cord.

Wrap the middle of one continuous sling around the corresponding C2 joist and
bring one end to each lower post. Set and mark both end knots while the corner
is held at its required height. Use the same upper-face stop principle as the
straight joists to prevent the midpoint migrating toward the tip.

For the first corner sample, use two turns around each smooth post followed by
a compact buntline hitch, generous tail and separate stopper. This is a
prototype starting detail, not acceptance by knot name: dress it consistently
and retain it only if the 0.30 kN dry and wet tests show no tail movement,
glazing or jamming against the retaining washer.

Two lower points are preferred to one central point because they introduce the
support directly into both corner legs and eliminate a separate lower bridge.
Their real geometry will increase the horizontal component somewhat because
the points lie 55 mm along the rail legs. Measure the C2 upper tangent, lower
post tangents and deck clearance, then model the two sling legs and a fault
removing the whole continuous sling before fixing the cutting length.

Do not drill dedicated cord holes in the first corner. Existing bolt posts are
cheaper, avoid new laminate bearing and let the cord turn around a replaceable
smooth surface. Reconsider radiused and sleeved plate holes only if the bolt-
post prototype creates an actual clearance or chafe problem.

## Prototype quantities

Buy only enough material for one gravity loop, one crossed pair and one corner
sling before the batch.

| Item | Provisional full-system allowance |
| --- | ---: |
| 6 mm accepted polyester cord | measured loop schedule plus 20% and two longest-loop spares |
| Main-cord chafe sleeves | 76 bearing locations plus sample-derived spare allowance |
| 3-4 mm polyester locating cord | two collars at each straight/crossed lower station plus spare |
| Treated upper-face timber stops | 32 straight/crossed stations plus 4 C2 stations |
| Stainless stop screws | two per stop plus 10% |
| Smooth corner cord-post spacers and retaining washers | 8 of each plus 2 spare |
| Crossing sleeves | 8 plus 2 spare |
| Loose crossed-cord separators | 4 plus 2 spare |
| Weatherproof numbered tags | 36 plus spares |

Final cord, stop, screw, sleeve and collar quantities come from the three
accepted full-size samples and the surveyed deck-board detail.

## Installation and proof

1. Survey the joists and mark provisional wrap/stop stations. Trial the stop
   screws in equivalent treated timber before drilling the installed members.
2. Assemble and proof one gravity loop, one crossed pair and one complete C2
   corner sling over dry ground. Use the production cord, sleeves, collars,
   posts, knots and stops.
3. Support and level the bare ring on temporary rigid blocks or slings. Install
   all loops without asking a knot or stop to hold the ring during adjustment.
4. Remove the temporary support gradually while checking ring level, corner
   square, loop sharing and every witness mark. Retie only while the temporary
   supports again carry the rail.
5. Proof each completed support path to 0.30 kN over dry ground. At the corner,
   test the two legs together, plausible unequal sharing and loss of the whole
   continuous sling.
6. Build the representative complete side and test the required symmetric,
   uneven, eccentric, support-loss and basket-removal cases.
7. Soak the lower wraps and repeat after 24 hours and seven days. Record knot
   bedding, tail movement, collar movement, chafe, timber indentation and ring
   level.
8. Fit the relieved removable deck boards only after every wrap, knot, stop and
   witness mark remains inspectable and replaceable.

Reject or revise a detail if a knot or tail moves more than 2 mm, a lower wrap
walks past a locating collar, an upper wrap loads its stop continuously, cord
or sleeve glazes/cuts/flattens, timber splits or indents materially, a corner
post bends or snags, or the ring cannot be restored accurately to its required
height. Inspect after the first month, after the first drain-down and at least
annually.
