# Planting and Basket schedule

This is the working procurement and assembly specification for the planted elements in Design C. It consolidates the final plant order in the [design discussion](https://chatgpt.com/share/6a983593-962c-83ed-b6dc-8478b204f2a2), including the later restoration of watercress and the change from soft rush to hard rush.

The thirteen rail baskets bear on the submerged carrier described in
[rail.md](rail.md). Use [R3_guide.md](R3_guide.md) to purchase, build and lower
the empty carrier and baskets before following the planting assembly below.
The lily, hornwort cage and willow-moss supports are independent of that rail.

## Design intent

The composition keeps one connected, dark open-water centre while planting the perimeter in layers. Two hard-rush clumps provide repeated tall, winter-green corners; the single white *Butomus* is an offset summer accent; low flowering and grass-like marginals soften the side edges; and one controlled lily supplies the surface layer. Baskets remain submerged and visually recessive, but crowns and foliage must reach open sky rather than being trapped beneath the 350 mm deck overhang. The current 2,100 mm rail square centres them 450 mm from the pond walls—100 mm beyond the deck edge—so the centred 150 mm planting pots have approximately 25 mm of open-sky clearance.

Hard rush is the final procurement choice and supersedes the earlier soft-rush table. It keeps the hardy evergreen corner structure but has a stiffer, more architectural blue-grey appearance. Preserve at least **65–70% of the plan** as one connected central swimming area, approximately **70–80% of the surface** as open water and approximately **80–90% of the floor** as unobstructed cleaning and swimming space.

The biological design is deliberately mixed rather than dependent on one plant: submerged foliage, persistent roots and removable fast growth all contribute. This planting supplements the biological filter, mechanical waste removal and independent aeration; it does not replace them. Harvest watercress, mint, crowfoot and hornwort and remove the cut material from the pond, otherwise much of the captured nutrient returns as it decays. Keep lily pads to about 20–30% of the surface and preserve the clear central swimming and cleaning route.

## Custom rail-seated baskets

### What the basket does

The rigid basket is both planter and serviceable root-volume boundary. A
separate fabric planting pocket is not required: duplicating a rigid basket
with a structural pocket adds cost, traps debris and complicates lifting. The
basket base bears across two transverse GRP crossbars; the plastic rim and top
mesh carry no rail load.

Four removable cord ties retain each basket at its lower corners. The support
remains serviceable because those ties can be undone or cut and replaced. See
[R3_guide.md](R3_guide.md) for the bearing datum, crossbars, B23 risers,
joist-support loops and the empty-frame installation.

### Assembly

1. Rinse and pre-soak the LECA. Wash the gravel until the rinse water is clear.
2. Inspect the basket base and identify two reinforced bearing bands near its
   opposite edges. Mark four sound lower-mesh lashing positions, one near each
   base corner. Reject a basket whose base deforms markedly across the proposed
   rail seat.
3. Cut a basket-top panel from the 5–6 mm HDPE mesh. Round every cut edge. Fit
   the geotextile liner inside the net pot without blocking the surrounding
   basket's water exchange.
4. Part-fill the rigid outer basket with LECA and a provisional, evenly spread
   amount of gravel. Seat the net pot on the aggregate at the height needed for
   its plant's crown. Its broad rim and local lacing prevent tilt/uplift, but the
   flexible top panel alone must not carry the filled pot.
5. Lace the top panel continuously to the rigid basket at approximately
   30–50 mm intervals and around the net pot. The panel contains LECA and
   excludes koi; use a small raised mesh collar where the pot/crown projects
   above the outer rim.
6. Wet the aquatic loam before filling the lined pot. Install the plant at the
   depth/orientation in the schedule and cover exposed loam with washed gravel
   beneath the guard. Keep the crown opening only as large as necessary.
7. Soak the complete assembly in a test tub and release all trapped air. Add
   gravel in approximately 250 g increments until a small basket has about
   **0.5–1.5 kg apparent downward weight underwater**. For B28 tall-plant
   prototypes, test whether **2–3 kg** is needed to resist wind-induced
   rocking; do not add it by rule if the lighter basket is stable.
8. Record the plant, basket ID, target crown depth, net-pot height above/below
   the outer rim, underwater apparent weight and **fully soaked out-of-water
   mass**. The latter governs rail/deck loading if the pond is drained. The
   rail's 100 N (approximately 10 kg) per-basket screen is only provisional.
9. Put B28 directly on its two crossbars. Put B23 on the two measured risers
   specified in [R3_guide.md](R3_guide.md). Lace each basket to the crossbars at
   all four marked base positions, level it, and proof-test one complete B23 and
   B28 over dry ground before batch assembly.

### Independent planted elements

- **L35 lily:** omit LECA. Fill with wet aquatic loam, retain the supplied rootball, keep the growing point exposed, and cap with washed gravel and a removable mesh guard. Use a stable removable riser to change depth.
- **H70 hornwort cage:** fold and lace the heavy HDPE mesh into a closed 30 × 30 × 70 cm column with a removable top. Put smooth slate weights in four internal bottom-corner pockets and stand it on smooth inert 5–10 cm feet.
- **M20 moss slates:** smooth every edge and tie the moss with pond-safe thread or cord. Do not bury it.

## Inspection and maintenance

- Weekly in the growing season: prevent watercress from matting across open water; remove loose/dead plant material.
- Seasonally: harvest excess watercress, mint, crowfoot and hornwort; remove dead *Butomus* foliage; keep the lily within its surface-area limit.
- Annually: inspect top mesh, pot restraint, basket base, crossbar bearing,
  risers, chafe protection and all four seat ties; lift baskets enough to check
  concealed faces.
- Every few years, or when visibly congested: undo or cut the four replaceable
  seat lashings, move the basket inward past the deck edge, recover it and
  divide the marginal.
