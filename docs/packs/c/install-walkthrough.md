# Pack C rail — prototype construction and installation guide

## Scope and current approach

This is the sole active fabrication, assembly, installation and test procedure
for the Pack C carrier. Use it with the dimensions and proof envelopes in
[R5.2.md](R5.2.md), [corner-v2.md](corner-v2.md), [cords.md](cords.md), and the
materials and listings in [purchase-cost-study.md](purchase-cost-study.md).

The seat's longitudinal stop uses **clamping pressure from its four bolts,
split upper bridge and two lower bridges**. Trial the plain clamp first; a
broad grippy pad or suitable adhesive between the bridge and rail is optional.
Do not cut seat-lashing slots or cord grooves for this approach. Suspension
cords, locating collars and corner slings are unchanged.

The assembly remains a prototype. Final sleeve fit, tightening procedure,
optional interface material and measured support/deck loads must be resolved
on samples before installing the loaded ring. A successful fortnight supports
continued inspected use; it is not proof of indefinite durability or unverified
deck capacity.

## 1. Prepare the work and installation area

1. Establish the real basket positions, rail height and minimum water level.
   Measure one B23 and one B27: outside top, reinforced base, continuous bearing
   bands/ribs, loaded deformation, taper, four possible retention zones and
   cartridge removal envelope. Product names are not cutting dimensions.
2. Identify sound support/anchor points and a temporary support arrangement
   that can hold the empty ring level, control its rotation during insertion,
   and support it again during adjustment or recovery. Check the temporary
   load path as well as the final distributed suspension.
3. Provide the required reusable slings/bearers, tools, dust extraction,
   backing blocks, mixing equipment and PPE. These are not new permanent rail
   components, but they must actually be available. Do not assume the cord
   reel's spare allowance also covers all installation equipment.
4. Work over dry ground. Keep GRP dust, swarf, uncured resin and loose hardware
   out of the pond. Keep people clear of the ring's fall/rotation area.

## 2. Cut, drill and seal the GRP

1. Measure all three 200 x 9.5 x 3,000 mm flat bars. Confirm thickness, useful
   width, straightness, fibre direction and supplier wet-service information.
   The lower corner web is about 197.48 mm wide in its stock orientation, so
   reject an edge-damaged or undersized bar rather than consuming its margin.
2. Follow the retained orientation-controlled Deepnest layouts. Keep every
   long seat member and both corner profiles in their specified pultrusion
   direction; do not rotate a part across the bar merely to save stock.
3. Select the 250, 225 or 200 mm R5.2 set from the measured reinforced base.
   Cut one sample set first: two 40 mm arms, one 90 mm bridge and two 35 mm
   lower bridges. Overall lengths are listed in R5.2. Do not cut lashing slots
   or grooves.
4. Cut one complete corner first: two mirrored upper plates, one thick lower
   web and one broad 9.5 mm offcut levelling pad. Enlarge the two weak outer
   M6 basket lands so every axis has at least 16 mm to the GRP edge, preferably
   20 mm. Do not introduce a sharp local tab to obtain that land.
5. Drill with backing support; round and deburr all edges and holes. Trial-fit
   before sealing, then seal exposed fibres with a compatible system. Recheck
   fit after cure; sealing must not prevent bolts or sleeves seating correctly.
6. Follow the product's full cure and pre-immersion instructions. Handling or
   demould time is not full cure. EL2 is the costed edge sealer, not automatically
   the optional structural adhesive described below.

## 3. Assemble the rail corners

1. Temporarily clamp the mitred rails and the thick lower webs in a flat square
   jig. Place the two mirrored upper plates in their final overlap and mark all
   six matched rail axes. Confirm the 55/125/195 mm pattern before drilling.
   Check the 2,100 mm centreline square and its diagonals; drill the matched
   stacks, then separate them for cleaning, sealing and sleeve insertion.
2. Feed the 24 internal crush sleeves through the open mitred ends. Match
   their lengths to the actual tube cavity and use the bolts/removable guides
   to hold them aligned. Do not enlarge the GRP bolt holes to admit a sleeve.
3. At each corner, put the rail-side upper plate directly over the rail and the
   C2-side plate above it. Use M8 x 90 bolts at the two shared 55 mm axes and
   M8 x 80 bolts at the four single-upper 125/195 mm axes. Put broad washers
   only at the outside faces, not between GRP plies. Check the real plain-shank,
   washer, nyloc and two-thread engagement before accepting either length.
4. Assemble all four corners loosely before final tightening. Keep access to
   the mitres until every sleeve is located; do not finish three corners and
   then try to insert sleeves into an inaccessible fourth corner.
5. Recheck square and tighten in a crossing sequence using the procedure
   established on the corner sample. Do not credit an assumed friction force
   from a generic M8 tightening torque. Mark each bolt/nut position.
6. Seal exposed mitre fibres while retaining deliberate flooding and drainage
   paths. Verify every hollow rail can release air and drain; do not create
   a sealed air chamber.
7. Before making the other three corners, proof the complete sample dry and
   after the specified wet dwell. Apply the retained 60 N m plan moment, 30 N m
   roll case, realistic eccentric basket action, deliberately unequal C2-leg
   action and complete 0.30 kN C2 proof action. Record bedding separately and
   confirm at least 20 kN m/rad stiffness over 0 to 60 N m after bedding.
8. Mark the two upper plates, lower web, rail legs, sleeves, bolts and nuts so
   connection slip can be distinguished from rail flexure. Reject progressive
   slip, whitening, hole ovalisation, washer dish, tube crushing, split-ply
   movement or loss of drainage.

## 4. Fit the seats and establish the clamped stop

### Identify the parts

Each seat has two transverse **upper arms**, one longitudinal **upper bridge**
and two **lower bridges**. At each capture station, two bolts pass through an
arm, the common upper bridge and one lower bridge, with a shaped POM-C tube
sleeve around each bolt beside the rail. Together the five GRP parts
mechanically capture the rail.

Basket gravity bears through the arms and upper bridge onto the rail. The
bolts and bridges retain the seat against uplift and overturning. Clamping,
with an optional pad or adhesive, prevents movement **along** the rail; none
of these interface options replaces the four-bolt capture.

### Establish one working sample

1. Use two 1 m lengths of 20 OD x 10 ID unfilled POM-C tube. Before cutting
   individual sleeves, support each full tube in a straight anti-rotation jig
   and form one continuous flat along it. Remove 1.0 mm radially so the finished
   dimension from the flat to the opposite circumference is 19.0 mm. Measure
   at intervals; do not freehand each short sleeve. Use a rigid abrasive bed,
   light cool passes and dust collection so the acetal does not heat and smear.
2. Lightly round the two longitudinal edges of the flat without reducing its
   bearing width. Cut the sleeves slightly long, square their ends, and finish
   all four in a seat as a matched set. The final length is established by the
   clamp sample below, not by copying the former nominal 39 mm.
3. Assemble one complete seat on a representative rail offcut with the actual
   washers, sleeves and M8 x 90 bolts. Put each upper arm above the upper bridge
   and keep broad GRP-to-GRP contact without an interlayer washer. Keep the
   basket off initially for access. Orient every 19 mm flat against the rail;
   the untouched round face remains outward.
4. Measure and record the rail height, plate/washer thicknesses and sleeve
   lengths. The former 39 mm sleeve around a nominal 38 mm rail intentionally
   left clearance; it is **not a final clamp dimension**.
5. Match the sleeve-controlled gap to the actual chosen interface. Establish
   broad, even upper-bridge-to-rail and lower-bridge-to-rail contact. Retain
   the sleeves as transverse guides and closure limits, but do not let
   overlong sleeves take all the bolt force before the rail is gripped.
6. Tighten the pairs evenly in small increments. Do not compensate for a loose
   fit by forcing the tube to flatten or by heavily compressing the acetal.
   There is no released universal sleeve shortening or tightening torque.
7. Check the complete bolt grip and nylon-lock engagement at the accepted
   stack. Record the fitted dimensions and repeatable tightening procedure.
   Make the remaining seat sets only after this sample passes the tests below.

### Optional grippy pad

If the plain clamp needs improvement, trial broad, firm pads between the upper
bridge's rail-contact regions and the rail. Use an identified material
suitable for the pond exposure; do not use point contacts, teeth or balls to
indent the GRP.

The rail plus the uncompressed pad thickness must exceed the sleeve-controlled
gap sufficiently for the pad to compress when assembled. The sleeves then
limit closure. This controls displacement, not a known clamping force: confirm
grip by testing rather than assuming the pad supplies it. Check for squeeze-out,
settlement, trapped grit, rocking and interference with basket removal.

### Optional adhesive

Alternatively, bond the upper bridge's rail-contact regions to the rail with
an adhesive supported for the actual GRP materials and permanent immersion.
Retain all four bolts, sleeves and both bridges. The bond provides longitudinal
restraint; normal gravity still bears through the plate and the mechanical
capture remains.

Accept the removal consequence before bonding: moving or removing the upper
bridge may require breaking the bond and inspecting/repairing the surfaces. Follow the
adhesive maker's preparation, bondline and full-cure instructions; keep adhesive
out of bolt holes and off the sleeves, nuts and inspection points. Do not assume
the already-priced EL2 sealer is a suitable adhesive, or bond a pad on both
sides without checking the resulting interfaces. Adhesive/pad purchases are
additional unless a suitable existing material is available.

### Accept the stop detail before batch assembly

- Mark the upper bridge against the rail, the nuts against their bolts, and
  any pad edges or visible bond boundaries. Measure seat migration separately
  from rail motion.
- Test with an empty basket and with the realistic wet basket/cartridge load.
  Weight must not disguise an ineffective clamp.
- Apply 100 N along the rail in each direction for five cycles, then hold
  100 N for ten minutes each way. Record initial bedding separately; accept
  at most 1 mm total permanent migration and no progressive slipping or damage.
- Apply the retained 0.30 kN seat action centred, 50 mm eccentric and at a
  deliberately localised bearing point, followed by uplift and a one-fastener-
  loss basket-retention case. Check both lower bridges and every M6 land rather
  than crediting load sharing that was not observed.
- Repeat after seven and fourteen days wet under representative load. Reject
  whitening, cracking, tube flattening, sleeve shortening, loosening, pad
  extrusion or adhesive peeling. Do not simply tighten harder after a failure.
- Open the mechanical sample after testing and inspect the hidden contact
  surfaces. For a bonded sample, inspect the bond edges and use a sacrificial
  bonded coupon to examine hidden damage without pretending the bond is reusable.

## 5. Fit baskets and finish fasteners

1. Retain four broad internal washers and M6 fixings per basket at sound rib
   locations, with underside washers and locking nuts. Check that the basket
   rests on its intended bearing areas and can be removed as planned.
2. At every corner, centre the basket at the rail intersection and rotate it
   45 degrees. Fit the sealed 9.5 mm GRP offcut pad beneath the one low bearing
   point so the basket rests on a common plane; do not pull it across the step
   with the M6 fastener. Keep B23 at the corner where the planting schedule
   allows.
3. Before assigning a B27 to a corner, make a full-size template containing
   the 270 mm basket, both C2 posts, 30 mm roof washers, sleeved cord turns,
   knots and cartridge. The nominal 275 mm post spacing is not usable clearance.
   If only an unloaded plastic corner conflicts, remove the cartridge and
   either trim that non-structural basket corner or form a smooth reinforced
   opening in the plastic basket for a separately sleeved cord. Do not cut the
   GRP plate, weaken a load-bearing basket rib, pinch the cord or alter its
   force line. Wet-test retention, snagging, cartridge removal and one-corner
   uplift before accepting the modification.
4. Trial-fit and mark excessive M6/M8 bolt tails. Remove them, cut and round
   the ends away from the pond, and restore clean thread entry with suitable
   tools. Do not use a nylon locknut as a thread-cleaning die.
5. Reassemble with full locking-nut engagement. Check final locking action
   after trial disassembly; replace ineffective nuts. Sheltered, smooth ends
   replace bought caps only if the actual bag/liner snag check passes.

## 6. Fit the suspension and corner slings

1. Make the gravity-loop, crossed-loop and C2 sling samples described in
   [cords.md](cords.md). Establish knots, tails and actual wrap tangents before
   cutting the batch. Retain generous tails until wet adjustment is complete.
2. Fit the eight separate ready-bored 16 OD x 8.4 ID x 20 mm nylon C2 spacers
   and retaining washers. Keep these posts fully round; do not substitute the
   larger flattened seat-sleeve tube.
   Verify the knot, sleeved turns, rotated basket rim, any accepted plastic-
   basket relief and deck-clearance envelope.
3. Start with **150 mm of chafe sleeve per C2 post**, not 100 mm. Two turns
   require protection along the rope's curved centreline, plus turn/exit
   allowance; the first dressed sample governs the final length.
4. Fit the upper timber stops and establish relieved removable-board access
   before loading the suspension. Keep all knots and witness marks accessible.

## 7. Insert and suspend the empty assembly

1. Remove planted cartridges and, where needed for handling clearance, empty
   baskets. Support the ring at the agreed control points.
2. If decking is installed, tilt and rotate in two axes through the opening.
   Check the complete swept path of pods, posts and remaining fittings, not
   just the flat footprint. Do not bear on the liner or force a snagged part.
3. Bring the ring level on its temporary supports. Install all suspension
   items and locating collars before transferring load to them.
4. Release temporary support gradually while checking level, square, sharing
   and witness marks. Secure the ring again before adjusting any loaded knot.
5. Retain access to the support/recovery arrangement during the trial. Any
   catch lines must have a checked load path and must not create entanglement
   hazards or be mistaken for part of the normal suspension.

## 8. Load, soak and check recovery

1. Verify that the hollow rails flood completely. Add clean, secured test
   loads progressively and symmetrically before testing uneven loading.
2. Match the installed baskets' **apparent underwater weight**. Submerged
   water-filled containers supply little net ballast. Separately check the
   fully soaked drained load if baskets can remain attached during drain-down.
3. Record ring levels, seat positions and knot tails initially, after 24 hours,
   and at seven and fourteen days. Inspect stops, sleeves, pads/bonds, fasteners
   and chafe surfaces. Re-support the ring before wet-bedding adjustments;
   repeat the relevant checks after adjustment.
4. Test cartridge removal/replacement and demonstrate unloading and replacing
   one slack support with the ring independently secured. Do not deliberately
   release a loaded support without a checked catch and recovery arrangement.
5. Fit/refit the relieved deck boards only when inspection and replacement
   remain possible. Keep the agreed access points identifiable.
6. Repeat the accepted local cases on one complete side, then on the complete
   ring with unequal basket and suspension loading. Compare measured rail,
   corner and deck/support movement with the local samples; a passing isolated
   seat or corner does not release the assembled system.

## Continued inspected use

The prototype may remain in place after the loaded fortnight if the local and
whole-system checks pass, the actual support/deck load allowance is established,
and adjustment/removal have been demonstrated. Continued suspension alone is
not a pass. Unload and investigate progressive movement, looseness, cracking,
chafe, pad settlement or bond separation.

Inspect again after the first month and first drain-down, then at least
annually and after unusual loading. The two-week trial does not establish
seasonal or long-term material behaviour.

## Purchase implications

The active purchase schedule is GBP 552.29 including VAT excluding delivery:
no seat-lashing cord, chafe pieces, slots or buckles are needed.
The locating-cord reel is still needed for the suspension. With seat-stop
chafe removed and C2 post sleeves increased to 150 mm, the webbing allocation
is **14.84 m of the purchased 20 m**, leaving 5.16 m for trials and spare.
Optional pads/adhesive, replacement trial nuts and any missing installation
equipment require a separate cost allowance; they are not silently included.
