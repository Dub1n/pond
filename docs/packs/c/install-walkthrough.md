# Pack C rail — prototype construction and installation guide

## Scope and current approach

This is the active construction, assembly, installation and test procedure for
the Pack C carrier. Use it with [R5.3](R5.3.md),
[corner v3](corner-v3.md), [cords](cords.md), the
[aluminium cutting schedule](aluminium-cut-parts.md), and the
[purchase study](purchase-cost-study.md).

The current assembly uses one-piece 10 mm aluminium upper plates, separate
10 mm aluminium lower pieces and 38 x 38 x 3.2 mm GRP rail. The seat's
longitudinal stop comes from its broad clamped interface with the rail. Do not
cut lashing slots or cord grooves. The design remains a prototype until the
material, completed joints, wet behaviour and deck reactions pass the stated
checks.

## 1. Survey and prepare

1. Measure the B23 and B27 baskets: reinforced base bands, loaded deformation,
   four sound retention zones, taper, cartridge fit and removal envelope.
   Product names and nominal rim sizes are not cutting dimensions.
2. Establish the real rail height, minimum water level and sound deck supports.
   Provide a temporary support that can hold and control the complete empty
   ring during insertion, adjustment and recovery.
3. Keep people clear of the ring's fall and rotation area. Work over dry ground
   and keep aluminium swarf, GRP dust and loose hardware out of the pond.

## 2. Receive the aluminium parts and rail

1. Give the fabricator [aluminium-cut-parts.md](aluminium-cut-parts.md) and its
   four linked SVGs. Order five 670 x 460 x 10 mm plates, subject to confirming
   EN AW-5083, the H temper, actual thickness and certificate.
2. On receipt, check each of the 35 parts against its SVG at 1:1 scale. Confirm
   hole count and diameter, overall dimensions, flatness and part identity.
   Reject scaling, mirroring, omitted holes or fabricator reinterpretation.
3. Confirm every edge and hole is deburred and lightly broken. Clean off swarf
   and cutting residue. Keep the stainless/aluminium isolation detail intact;
   do not assume paint, anodising or sealant alone makes a wet joint safe.
4. Measure both 38 x 38 x 3.2 mm GRP rail lengths, including actual wall and
   cavity dimensions. Cut and dry-fit the ring, round exposed edges and seal
   cut GRP ends without creating trapped air or water.

## 3. Assemble and prove one corner

1. Jig two mitred rails square beneath one `40mm_web.svg` upper and one
   `corner-web-lower.svg` lower. The six matched rail axes are at 55, 125 and
   195 mm along each leg. There is no bolt at the rail-axis intersection.
2. Make six stainless crush sleeves to the measured rail cavity at each
   corner—24 total. Feed them through the open ends and use bolts or removable
   guides to keep them aligned. Do not enlarge the GRP holes to admit a sleeve.
3. Assemble each of the six M8 rail axes with outside-face 25 mm washers and a
   nyloc. Install the two separate C2 posts with their purchased M8 x 50 bolts,
   nylon spacers, 30 mm roof washers and 25 mm underside washers.
4. Use the purchased longer rail bolts where necessary. Check the real stack,
   plain-shank/thread transition, full nyloc engagement and at least two clear
   threads, then trim only surplus threaded tip and deburr it. Trimming does
   not move the thread transition toward the head.
5. Tighten by the repeatable crossing procedure established on the sample;
   mark every bolt, nut, sleeve, plate and rail so bedding and slip can be
   distinguished. Keep all cavities drainable.
6. Test the complete corner dry and after representative wet dwell: 60 N m
   plan moment, 30 N m roll, eccentric basket action, unequal C2 legs and loss
   of one C2 sling. After bedding it must demonstrate at least 20 kN m/rad
   from 0 to 60 N m, with no progressive slip, hole ovalisation, aluminium
   deformation, rail crushing or isolation damage. Pass this sample before
   assembling the other three corners.

## 4. Assemble and prove one R5.3 seat

1. Identify one `40mm-r512.svg` upper and two
   `40mm-r512-bridge.svg` lower bridges. The four inner upper holes and both
   lower pairs form the M8 rail capture; the four outer upper holes take the
   retained M6 basket fasteners.
2. Prepare four unfilled POM-C sleeves from 20 OD x 10 ID tube. Form one
   continuous rail-facing flat 1.0 mm deep before cutting individual pieces,
   giving 19.0 mm from flat to opposite face. Finish four matched lengths from
   a real clamped sample; do not copy an unverified nominal sleeve length.
3. Assemble the one-piece upper above the rail and one lower bridge at each
   station. Put a sleeve beside the rail around each M8 bolt and broad washers
   only at the outside aluminium faces. Tighten evenly without crushing the
   rail or using sleeve shortening to manufacture preload.
4. Verify the purchased bolt grip as in the corner assembly. Trim and deburr
   excess threaded ends only after the complete sample has passed.
5. Apply 100 N along the rail in both directions and record initial movement,
   bedding and residual migration. Acceptance is no progressive slip and no
   more than 1 mm permanent migration. If the plain interface fails, develop
   and qualify a broad, thin, wet-compatible isolation/grip layer; it may
   supplement but never replace the four-bolt capture.
6. Test 0.30 kN centred, 50 mm eccentric, local-bearing and uplift cases dry
   and after wet dwell. Reject permanent set, plate damage, loose fasteners,
   rail damage, sleeve extrusion or progressive movement before making the
   remaining eight seats.

## 5. Fit and prove the baskets

1. Overlay each real reinforced base and washer footprint on its upper before
   drilling a basket. Use all four as-drawn outer holes and sound basket ribs.
2. Fit each basket with four M6 button-head fasteners, 40 mm internal washers,
   underside Form G washers and nylocs. The basket's service weight must bear
   on aluminium; the fasteners retain it during handling and uplift.
3. At corners, trial the complete basket with both C2 posts, roof washers,
   sleeved cords, knots, cartridge and removal travel present. Do not trim a
   structural aluminium load path to obtain basket clearance.
4. Prove the empty basket, representative wet/drained load, eccentric action,
   uplift, one-fastener loss, snag-free cartridge removal and full basket
   removal. Inspect the plastic ribs and all aluminium lands after the test.

## 6. Cordage, installation and commissioning

1. Follow [cords.md](cords.md). Make each direct 5 mm polyester support loop
   independently; 3 mm cord is locating cord only. Fit all webbing chafe
   sleeves and keep tails visible for inspection.
2. Assemble the empty ring on temporary supports. Confirm square, level,
   drainage, all marked fasteners and every planned support before lowering.
3. Load in stages: empty ring, empty baskets, wet media/cartridges, then plants.
   At each stage inspect level, rail movement, corner bedding, cord extension,
   chafe, deck response and removal access. Stop on progressive change.
4. Record an as-built schedule of part IDs, plate certificate, rail dimensions,
   sleeve lengths, bolt grips, tightening marks, support coordinates, proof
   results and photographs.

## 7. Inspection and service

Inspect after the first fill, after 24 hours, after one week and after two
weeks; thereafter set an interval from observed change and recheck after major
plant maintenance or water-chemistry change. Look for loose marks, aluminium
pitting or crevice attack, isolation failure, GRP whitening/crushing, sleeve
movement, cord wear, knot movement, basket cracking and loss of level.

The ring must remain recoverable onto temporary supports. Remove the affected
basket or unload and lift the ring if any change is progressive. A successful
fortnight supports continued inspected prototype use; it is not evidence of
indefinite durability or unverified deck capacity.
